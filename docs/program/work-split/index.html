How the split was made

  1. Machine and testbeds first. Anything that needs the Windows PC stays with Bilal: its Docker, its GPU, its slot lock, the frozen experiment images, the raw transcripts, and anything inside the clinic testbed or FixFlow. The teammate works only in this repository and on a testbed she builds herself (WP13).
  2. She builds, he ports. She builds each check or script as a reusable piece and proves it on her testbed. Bilal wires it into the clinic check or FixFlow. Porting is counted in his days.
  3. The person who builds is not the person who judges. Bilal writes WP8's frozen validation contract, and she builds the library and retrieval pilots it judges. She is the contract's fresh reader before it is frozen, and he reviews her scripts before any of them may refuse anything.
  4. Strengths. Her CV shows Docker, CI/CD and GitHub Actions; queues (BullMQ, Celery/Redis); schema-validated staged LLM pipelines (Zod, Pydantic) with retry; RAG with persistent per-user context; tree-sitter AST mining; PyTorch; transaction integrity in a multi-tenant fintech backend; React/Next.js. Bilal keeps the record's history, FixFlow's domain, his account's settings, the GPU run and the live trials.
  5. Sizes. Sizes are in person-days of focused work. E means a design estimated the size; G means it is a guess until the first step reports. Work in the "next round" list is assigned but not sized: each item is sized after the gate named for it.
