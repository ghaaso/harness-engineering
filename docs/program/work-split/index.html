How the split was made

  1. Machine first. Anything that needs the Windows PC stays with Bilal: its Docker, its slot lock, its logs, the frozen experiment images, or the raw transcripts. Raw conversations do not leave the PC (the repository's exclusion rule), so none of the teammate's packages depends on them.
  2. The person who builds is not the person who judges. Bilal writes WP8's frozen validation contract. The teammate builds the library, knowledge-store and retrieval pilots that the contract judges. She is the fresh reader of the contract before it is frozen, and he reviews her scripts before any of them may refuse anything.
  3. Strengths. Her CV shows Docker, CI/CD and GitHub Actions; queues (a BullMQ priority queue, Celery/Redis workers); staged LLM pipelines with schema-validated output (Zod, Pydantic) and retry; RAG with persistent per-user context; tree-sitter AST mining with fingerprint clustering; PyTorch training; React/Next.js dashboards. Bilal keeps the work that needs the record's history, FixFlow's domain, his own account's settings and the live trials.
  4. Split where the PC is needed. Three packages are split between the two. In WP2a she builds the container gate and he measures it on the PC. In WP3 she writes the scripts and he audits the transcripts. In WP10b she writes the CI check and he reviews the skills. WP7 has a closing step that only the PC can run (his) and a next deliverable (hers).
  5. Sizes. Sizes are in person-days of focused work. E means a design estimated the size; G means it is a guess until the first step reports. Bilal's decisions and FixFlow's P0a are not counted. His reviews and merges of her work are counted.
