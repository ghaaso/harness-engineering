The bottleneck rule, version 2

Version 1's frame stands: one calculation at every start and finish of a unit of work, run at the smallest number any resource can carry, act only at the gate. Research round 3 changed what goes into it. This page shows where each conclusion came from, what the refuters left of it, and what is still a guess.

Nothing on this page is yours to set. No number of the rule exists yet. Each one waits for a measurement, and no measurement starts without your word.

The answer

  1. The frame is kept. Added to it: the parent session's own window as a resource, one token row per window, and a second stop for a unit that reaches its own cap.
  2. A token window limits by fit and by order. A unit starts if it fits in what every window has left above a reserve. When a window cannot pay for everything, the most valuable work goes first. Starts are not capped at an even rate, as the draft had it. That change is mine, and a refuter then narrowed it.
  3. What starts next: a reservation for the oldest unit that does not fit, shorter ones behind it. A unit's model stays the one its kind of job is routed to.
  4. The check: past a computed number of workers, more workers only queue. Four levers raise that number. Which comes first is open.
  5. Several writers on one file: the switch between own copies and one owner is replaced by seven ranked parts. Reshaping the shared places comes first. No file gets one owner by default.
  6. The tree of sessions: the two-level tree stays for a small job. The largest expected gain is fewer wakes of the parent. Whether a leaf that outgrows its window is split or handed over is still open.
  7. All of it is untested for model sessions. Fifteen points are open, each with the measurement that settles it and with what the rule does until then.

Two changes I made to the draft

The draft was written and checked by child sessions. They did not have two things: my ruling on pacing from the same day, and your rulings on models. These two changes are mine. One fresh Opus refuter attacked them and found both too strong. They are shown here as narrowed; what it took out is at the end of this section.

Changed in this session

Starts are not capped at an even rate

The draft
From the moment a window's used share runs ahead of its share of time gone, hold starts back and spend the rest evenly up to the reset.
Why not
That rule comes from ad budgets, where better chances to spend keep arriving all day. For waiting work of equal value, spending at once finishes each unit sooner, and the same units by the reset. The refuter added a condition: only if no running unit is cut off when the window runs out. Where units differ in value, the published gain came from their order.
Decided
A unit starts if it fits in what every window has left above a reserve, and the reserve covers the running units up to their caps. When a window cannot pay for all waiting work, the most valuable per token goes first. Tokens are held back in two ways only: a share kept for work that must not stall and for your own use of the same windows, and a price once units have values.
Still a guess
Whether any pacer gains for us. The kept share has no number and no unit has a value, so until then only the reserve acts.

A sketch of the argument; its sizes are not measured. The same six units are done either way, as long as no running unit is cut off when the window runs out. Holding tokens back helps only work that arrives late.

Changed in this session

A unit's model is the one its kind of job is routed to

The draft
Pick the model with the highest value minus what its tokens cost in the windows, a redo counted. Until pass rates are measured, "the better model runs".
Why not
You ruled the smallest model that gives the same output, on routes that are enforced. No file of the lane, the draft or its refuter mentions those rulings. Read literally, "the better model" sends every unit to the largest model.
Decided
The routes stand, and the token window does not choose the model. The draft's formula stays a hypothesis about what measured pass rates would justify changing in the routes. The rule itself changes no route.
Still a guess
The routes rest on your ruling, not on a measurement: no record here compares a smaller model with a larger one. Pass rate, usage and redo by kind of work and model are unmeasured, and so is what each model charges in the windows.

Three points the draft left open

  • The second stop takes the form "commit, hand the rest over, end". Your ruling on handing over through files supports it by likeness only: it was made for a session at its context ceiling. No source gives the form.
  • No file gets one owner by default, because you rejected "one writer per file" as the coarsest lock. The refuter confirmed this one.
  • A leaf of the tree that outgrows its window: I first wrote that the tree's design hands it over. The refuter showed that the design's own rules disagree. It is open again, and nothing is built for it.

What the refuter did to my decision: of 21 points it confirmed 4, left 15 standing only in part, refuted 1 and could not check 1. Refuted: I had dropped version 1's cap on age from the order for units that need only a worker; it is back. Narrowed: "the same units by the reset" needed its condition on cut-off units. My way of holding tokens back used a rule that knows nothing of value. I had credited one of Yahoo's gains to pacing when it came from ranking by value. I had called the routes tested when no record compares models. I had called the hand-over the record's answer. Each is corrected on this page. One argument was added afterwards and nobody has checked it: an even-rate cap also slows a burst that would have fitted the window.

With several windows, a unit starts only if it fits in every window it draws on. Its price is the sum of their prices.

New
Version 1
One token row. The lane's first frame said the dearer window binds.
Round 3 read
Fit in every bucket is the rule of two-bucket network meters (the ATM specification). The summed price is revenue management's: accept only if the fare exceeds the sum of the prices of all capacity used (theory).
Refuters left
Holds in part. Fit is proven where a cost is known on arrival; we admit on an expected usage. The sum is exact only for costs that add up, and is not shown for windows that count the same tokens.
Still a guess
The size of each window, whether they count usage alike, and which windows a one-model unit draws on.

The windows: a 5-hour period that starts at the first message and resets in full, a weekly reset at a fixed time per account, on some plans a weekly window for one model.

Corrected

Listed "fixed or rolling" as a measurement to make.
Round 3 read
The provider's own pages, fetched on 19 November 2026. Every client counts toward the same windows.
Refuters left
Confirmed. The pages are undated and say limits may change.
Still a guess

What starts next

A reservation for the oldest unit that does not fit. Shorter units start behind it only if they do not delay it.

Corrected
Version 1
The shortest expected check first, then the oldest, with a cap on age.
Round 3 read
Backfilling on supercomputers. The improved form against the plain one: wait 23 % lower, bounded slowdown 37 % lower, on four real job logs replayed in a simulator with 100 to 1,152 processors.
Refuters left
Confirmed, as a transfer. Where every unit needs only a worker the rule shrinks to first come, first served, and nothing shows that order is good: shortest first lowered waits in the same studies. Version 1's order stays for that case, with its cap on age.
Still a guess
Whether it holds at 3 to 16 workers. Which goes first when a token window is the thing not free, the most valuable unit or the oldest: no claim ranks them.

The check every change must pass

Past c × (S + Z) / S workers, more workers only queue.

c: check slots. S: the time of one check run. Z: a worker's own time between two checks.

Corrected
Version 1
The same form, marked "a textbook bound, reasoned".
Round 3 read
Published for one slot (Denning and Buzen 1978; Lazowska 1984). For several slots no source read states it. Lane c1 computed it on the exact queueing model in 16 cases.
Refuters left
Confirmed. The refuter derived the same point from two bounds that hold for any spread of times. Changes already wait below it: in the standard model the check is 79 to 92 % busy there. More workers never move the point. A shorter or batched check does.
Still a guess
That two runs side by side keep full speed. Z was never measured. One suite run took 596 s, measured once.

Four levers raise that number: shorten the run, batch what waits, test ahead, let a waiting worker go on. Which comes first is open.

New
Version 1
"Make the check wider or shorter."
Round 3 read
Running only the affected tests: 32 % shorter on average, 54 % on long suites, up to 38 % slower on short ones. Batching: 41 to 72 % fewer runs at Ericsson (simulated). The best batch is 11 changes when 1 % fail and 3 when 30 % fail (Dorfman 1943). Testing ahead: Uber, its own account.
Refuters left
The lane's ranking failed. The one shortening open at our gate, test files side by side, is untested. The one study with few builds advises selection where builds are few and batching where machines are scarce, and we are both. A claim that one company keeps the full suite at its gate was refuted.
Still a guess
Whether our test files run side by side. How often a change fails the check: no gate exists yet.

Several writers on one file

Seven ranked parts replace the switch between own copies and one owner. Reshape the shared places first; version-checked writes second.

Replaced
Version 1
Per file, own copies or one owner, switched on a count of conflicts.
Round 3 read
Database engines, failure by failure. Taking the hot place out of the unit: 38 times faster on one hot key, 3 times on a larger application, for updates that commute. Version-checked writes on model sessions: 82.5 % of tasks passed, against 63.8 % for own worktrees and 66.4 % for one agent (one preprint, at most 4 writers, no repeated runs reported).
Refuters left
Holds in part. Every engine figure is for units of seconds or less. Reshaping removes the hot place's own conflicts; the chance of meeting another writer elsewhere still grows with the number of writers. A refused write is redone alone only when the refusal concerns that file alone.
Still a guess
Which of our 20 shared places can be reshaped: they were counted from the plan, not from diffs. Whether the Edit tool can carry a version check.

The tree of sessions

The kind of tree follows the size of the job. Small: the two-level tree as built. Very large: more coordinator rounds or an inner level, whichever costs fewer tokens.

New

Database index results. On agent hierarchies, one study spent 196 thousand tokens against 1,029 thousand with most delegations running at once.
Refuters left
Holds in part. That saving is in cache reads and covers only tasks both methods solved. A level that checks its children's reports may pay in errors, untested.
Still a guess
The tree never ran.

To set the numbers: 36 measurements

By what each one needs

  • Counts over transcripts, the lane log and history that already exist: no run6
  • Readings of the usage meter during ordinary work6
  • Simulations swept over project sizes4
  • Runs: on the PC, in the tree's pilot, or one probe13
  • Counts that wait for data: a gate, a tree run, a second model6
  • No known method: what a unit is worth1

The three that set the most numbers

  • Tokens, minutes and start cost per unit, counted from transcripts on disk. It sets the length predictions, the reserve and the landings asked per hour.
  • Check time and its spread, counted from the lane log. It sets the worker cap and the waits. The failure rate must wait for a gate to exist.
  • Each window's size and each model's weight, from the meter's change against counted tokens. It sets every token price.

Nothing here starts without your word. The planned order is counts first, then simulations swept over project sizes so that no number is tuned to one repository, then runs. The counts need the transcripts and logs on your PC; this session ran in the cloud and did not have them.

Not found in what was read

  • Any writer mechanism measured on units of minutes in a real engine, and the conflict rate at which locking and the optimistic method cross.

  • How long a half-finished split lasts in a tree, and depth against fan-out at equal work with tokens per level.
  • Never searched for, so not "not found": a team's account of hitting a usage limit, and scheduling with rough durations.
