===== claims :: holds-with-corrections
SUMMARY: The measurement holds; several claims drawn from it do not. Shares, groups and repeat buckets reproduce exactly, and thinking is carried in context, so the split is sound. Seven claims overstate or mis-cite.

- **C1:** the "at most 27 %" ceiling ignores that every lookup step re-reads the whole window. 54.6 % of calls were read/search-only, and they re-read 43.6 % of context tokens.
- **C5:** "needed" is unsupported. 74 % of fetched tokens went to files the window never edited.
- **C8:** "no small hot set" is false. 3.3 % of paths hold half the fetched tokens, and 294 paths read in 5 or more windows hold 73 %.
- **C12:** the 6-17 % comes from a Codex compaction snapshot-and-restore layer, not from any index or graph. The "no" stands on external evidence instead.
- **C11:** PR #183 is unbuilt, covers diagnosis entries only, and excludes work state, so it is not "already designed".
- **C7:** the 400k compaction figure is an exposure, not a saving.
- **C13:** the evidence supports a defect trial, not a token trial, and the co-change half is contra-indicated at 432 commits.
- **C6:** holds for slices only. Subagents read whole files more.
- **C10:** holds in part, but a lookup returned as a tool result does not break the cache.

Strongest case for the owner's idea:
- **Lookup calls re-read the window:** read/search-only calls re-read 43.6 % of context tokens, so cutting lookup steps saves whole windows, not just result bytes.
- **Subagents:** 40 % of their tokens are fetched, and 76.5 % of their calls are read/search-only.
- **A cross-window hot set:** the same files are fetched in many windows, which is what a hash-invalidated digest cache would target.

What cuts against it: repeat reads inside one window are only 0.5 % of tokens, and nothing has shown that a digest can stand in for the lines.
