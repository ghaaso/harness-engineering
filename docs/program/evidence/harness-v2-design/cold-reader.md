# Cold read of the Harness v2 page

Read through the browser pane. I clicked phase cards 2, 3 and 5 and read the panels. I did NOT click the six demo tabs (only the check-queue demo was showing, with its default settings), the problem tiles, the decision-card "To start / All" switch, or any number or dotted word. So claims about those controls are untested. The page reads as one long scroll; at 375 px wide it was a single narrow column.

## The ten questions

1. **What comes first, and why?** Phase 1, "Keep the work safe, get back up": a copy of the work off the PC, a restart that needs no commands, and a diagnosis of the power-offs. It comes first because the PC has powered off 24 times and the work exists only on that PC ("Nothing else matters until that is covered"). Found in: top summary ("so the machine comes first"), Part 2 phase cards, then the phase 1 panel (opens by default). Easy.

2. **Decisions before anything can start?** Six "To start" decisions in Part 4: D01 go ahead with the plan (start phase 1); D02 where the off-machine copy lives (encrypted external drive, private remote/cloud, or both); D03 let Docker and the databases restart themselves; D04 stop the PC sleeping while sessions hold work; D05 allow a sensor logger and say whether the house lost power on 16 listed days; D06 how sessions come back after a stop. Found in: Part 4 cards and the "Your decisions" tab at the top. Easy, but the phase 1 panel's own "You decide" list differs (see confusions).

3. **What does phase 2 fix?** The check (the automated test run). The Linux container run becomes the gate, the number of check slots is set from one measured memory number, a "heartbeat" shows waiting/running/stalled, a memory table says who holds memory, and a clean-up rule handles piled-up worktrees and containers. Found in: phase 2 card, then its panel ("What gets built", "Answers 7 of the 21"). Easy once the card is clicked.

4. **Not being built (four) and one admitted unsolved.** Not built: tree/forest/graph layers in prompts; debate loops; a knowledge graph extracted by a model; embeddings or a vector index (also: a second vendor's review pass, hooks, a vote over reviewers' findings). Admitted unsolved: how re-read tokens count against the plan's limit (also: the cause of the power-offs until diagnosed, switching the PC on while you are away, a human reviewer other than you, proof of load limits without a real host). Found in: Part 5, "What is not being built" and "What v2 does not solve". Easy (bottom of page).

5. **Kind of each number.** The page uses tags M measured, S setting, T target, E estimate, I illustrative.
   (a) Number of power-offs (24): measured (M).
   (b) Back to work in under three hours: target (T); the baseline "about three days" is measured. Found in the phase 1 panel, "How we will know it worked".
   (c) Wait of 2,030 s: measured (M); it is the "longest wait on record".
   (d) "About a week" to build phase 3: estimate (E), in the phase 3 "What it costs".
   (e) 400,000-token window: setting (S), in phase 5 ("Set the window to 400,000 tokens"). The 367k compaction point beside it is also S.
   (f) Number of sessions in the check-queue demo: illustrative (I); "Sessions wanting a check: 6" carries the I tag, and the demo is called a simulation.
   Easy, with the legend near the top. I could not verify that a tap shows a source.

6. **What caused the three-day stop in early November?** A power-off, not a usage limit. The PC powered off at 14:58:23 on 6 November, 31 seconds after the last commit, there was no limit message that day, and nothing restarts after a reboot, so everything stayed stopped until a person came back. Found in: Part 1 "Five facts" (second pair) and the top summary. Easy. Note: the page never says plainly "three days" next to the power-off explanation. The figure appears as "from about three daysM" in phase 1's proof list.

7. **Phase 3 mechanisms.** Eleven. Scripts that spend no tokens come first (sealed replay, verdict script, transcript audit, finding table, dry run with stand-in agents, merge queue = mechanisms 1 to 6). Then two are a line in a brief (7 and 8, pulled in from phase 1), then fix-round clauses (adds an agent), your reading of marked lines (your time) and a short tool list (a setting) come last. Each is accepted only by its own stated test, and for most that is a replay on the recorded history; scripts run "record-only" before they may refuse anything, and a script may refuse a merge only after you say so. Found in: phase 3 panel, "The eleven mechanisms, in order" plus "Rules that hold in every phase". Easy to find. "Tap one for its replay" I did not test.

8. **Does the page claim an earlier compaction window saves about 40 % of tokens?** No. I found no such figure. Phase 5 only says to set the window to 400,000 tokens for one wave and check that compactions happen near about 367k, that each extra compaction costs one full-context call, and that "The result will be an anecdote". It also says "No promise is made that fewer tokens means fewer stops." It is called a "weak" trial and is optional. Found in: phase 5 panel. Easy. (Elsewhere, "6 to 17 % more tokens" appears about failed context-machinery trials, a different thing.)

9. **Why are tokens last?** The three-day stop was a power-off, not a usage limit; limits did interrupt work on other days but how much is not measured; and nobody knows how re-read tokens count against the plan's limit, so no token cut can promise fewer stops. Also phase 3's tool changes alter session cost, so measure after. Found in: top summary and phase 5 panel, and "Why this order" (3 to 5). Easy.

10. **What stops the plan adding so many agents that work halts?** A single "one budget" rule set: two lanes at a time, a written token budget before any launch (figure set in phase 1), one agent-adding change per window, state on disk, scripts before agents, stop launching agents after the first empty result, and no harness agents while a FixFlow phase is running or waiting on its reviewer. The "How it stays inside one budget" section lists every agent-adding item with its cost. It also admits it "does not promise fewer" stops. Found in: "Rules that hold in every phase" and "How it stays inside one budget", both below the phase panels. Took some looking, because these sit under the panel, below the "Why this order" diagram.

## Things a non-technical owner would find confusing, missing, cluttered or hard to find (most important first)

1. **Jargon without a plain gloss in the main text.** "the root", "slice sessions", "lanes", "orchestrator", "gate", "worktrees", "compaction window", "P0a", "lock hold", "sealed replay", "stand-in agents", "record-only". Some may be dotted words (not tested), but the page text reads as insider shorthand, e.g. "Releasing two at a time does not make the work finish sooner... holding its context".
2. **Decision counts do not agree.** The top tab says "6 to start. 16 later", Part 4 says "22 cards... only six are needed to start", but phase 1 says "7 decisions" and "Five decisions now and two later", while its "You decide" list contains items (token budget per window, battery backup) that are not among the six start cards. D01 is also a "whole plan" yes, not a phase 1 choice.
3. **Very long page, with panels that replace each other.** The phase panel is followed by about eight sections ("Why this order", "Rules...", "How it stays inside one budget", "Where the ideas pull against each other") that are the same under every phase. A reader who clicks phase 5 still scrolls through all of them. I did not notice a clear "end of phase panel" marker.
4. **The budget question is buried.** The answer to "what keeps this from adding agents" is two sections down the page, under the phase 3 and 5 content, with no pointer from the top summary. The phase 5 panel is also a poor home for it, since the budget "applies from phase 1".
5. **Tag letters are everywhere and cluttered.** "SevenM", "24M", "twoM", "nineM", "about 306kM" fuse the tag onto the word, so they read as typos or odd words in text extraction. The legend says tags are M/S/T/E/I, but "OUR LEAN" and the ID codes "B05", "B03", "D01", "G4-F084", "PR #183" are unexplained.
6. **Internal bookkeeping leaks into an owner page.** "The compile's summary says six builds and four diagnoses. Its own tables say seven and three", "58 ideas that only fit FixFlow's ship plan", "(G4-F084)", "PR #183", "refuter" ("the refuter expects it to fail"). The owner has not been told what a "refuter" or "the compile" is.
7. **The first-screen summary is dense.** "SevenM wait on a decision from you, sevenM are ordinary builds, threeM need one measurement on this PC and fourM are trials" is a line of four numbers with no stated total, and "21 problems" is not defined for someone who has not read the earlier project.
8. **Unclear which demo controls respond and what a demo result means.** The check-queue demo shows "1,835 s" and "2,202 s" next to a real "2,030 s" line. The page says demos are not results, but the numbers sit beside real ones. Also, the "Did the house lose power on these days?" grid has 16 rows of Cut / No / ? with no visible save or send. I did not test whether answers carry into "Copy my answers".
