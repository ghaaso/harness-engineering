The decision rule, version 2

How the harness decides how much work runs at once, what starts next, and how several sessions share files, the check and the token allowance. Decided today from research round 3 and from the corrections made after its draft. It is still a hypothesis: nothing was run, and no number is set. Nothing on this page is yours to set.

Decided 2026-11-19 Hypothesis: not run 0 numbers set 38 conclusions: 8 kept, 9 corrected, 2 replaced, 19 new 145 checked statements: 86 confirmed, 53 in part, 1 refuted, 5 checked by search only

The answer

  • Same frame as version 1. At every start and finish, work out how many units each resource can carry now and run at the smallest. Act only at the gate where new work starts.
  • Two new kinds of limit. Each token window (the 5-hour period, the week, and a one-model week on some plans), and the coordinating session's own context window.
  • Tokens are paced only to protect later demand. While the queued work fits in what a window has left after your own expected use, units start at full speed. Until your use is measured, the draft's pacing stands in for it.
  • What starts next. Inside a short window, the most value per token first, and the oldest unit held back starts first at the reset. Waiting for memory or the check, a reservation for the oldest unit, with shorter units filling in behind it.
  • The check. Past c(S + Z)/S workers, more workers only queue. Its four levers (shorten, batch, test ahead, let a waiting worker go on) are not ranked yet; three measurements rank them.
  • Several writers. The per-file switch of version 1 is replaced by seven parts, reshaping shared places first and version-checked writes second. No file gets one owner by default.
  • The tree. Its shape follows the size of the job. The largest expected saving is fewer wakes of the parent session.
  • Every number is a measurement. 37 are named; 7 are counts over files already on disk and need no run.

Five things I decided beyond the draft

The draft was written and checked before the last session decided 31 corrections, so it did not carry them. These five come from those corrections and from a conflict between two parts of the draft. Rows they change are marked in the table below.

Decision 1 · rows 4, 5

A token window is paced only to protect later demand

The draft
From the moment the share of a window used runs ahead of the share of its time gone (u > s), starts are capped at (1 − u)/(1 − s) of the even rate.
Against it
Correction r57-0813: pacing's published gain comes from spreading spend over a day. With equal value per token, spending at once finishes the same units sooner. Bid-price control (Talluri and van Ryzin, read in the paper): accept a request only if its value is above the summed prices of what it uses, a price being what later demand would pay.
Position
Keep the reserve for running units, protect the later demand until the reset, and spend the rest at full speed while the queue fits. Our later demand is your own use of the same windows (every client counts) and work you order later. When the week ran out on 16 November your work stopped for about 12 hours.
Still a guess
How much later demand there is. A new count (Q37) gives it. Until then the draft's pacing stands in, because under "quality first" a stopped owner is the costlier error.
Decision 2 · row 9

Value order and the reservation no longer compete

The conflict
The draft says "most value per token first" while a window limits, and "a reservation for the oldest unit that does not fit" where a unit needs a share of a window. When a window limits, both apply and name different units.
Position
A reservation is the earliest time the held unit fits. For a window that resets in full, that time is the reset, and nothing spent before it delays a start at the reset. So inside the window, value per token orders the starts; the oldest unit held back starts first at the reset. For memory and the check, which units give back, the reservation stays as drafted.
Source
EASY backfilling's own definition of a reservation; the provider's pages on full resets (fetched 19 November).
Still a guess
All of it for sessions; it rests on published forms, not on a run.
Decision 3 · rows 22, 25

Own copies are a safety floor, not a quality claim

The draft
Said own worktrees led instruction-only separation by 3 points (CAID) and fell below one agent in STORM's rerun.
The record
Correction r57-0838: CAID put own worktrees above one agent, 59.1 against 53.1 % on Commit0-Lite, at about 2.3 times the time and 4.3 times the cost. STORM's rerun put them below, 63.8 against 66.4 %. Both are preprints with one run per arm.
Position
The floor stays: own copies, and every landing through the check in one order, with undo. It keeps one session's half-done edit out of another's check. Whether side-by-side work beats one agent is not settled by these two papers; running work together is your ruling, and what it costs in tokens is counted (Q01, Q02).

Decision 5 · row 3

One way to stop a unit at its cap

The draft
A unit that reaches its own cap commits what it has and ends; the draft found no source for this form.
Position
The stop is the rule's own checkpoint (Step 8, part 5) taken at the cap, then the session ends and a successor resumes from it, as the tree's hand-over at the window does. One mechanism then serves the stop at the cap, a refused write and a leaf that outgrows its window.
Still a guess
Designed in our own record only, never built. What a stop loses (the work since the checkpoint and the successor's reload) is counted in Q02 and Q30.
