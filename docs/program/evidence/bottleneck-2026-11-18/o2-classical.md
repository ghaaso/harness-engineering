## Gate summary
- P1 (Chekuri-Khanna 2004, read in main parts): with d scarce resources, list scheduling on the summed load is a (d+1)-approximation; best poly-time is O(ln^2 d), and for fixed d a PTAS; hard to approximate within any constant for arbitrary d. quoted, txt 86-92, 139-143, 486-495.
- P2 (Panigrahy et al. 2011, read in full): every first-fit-decreasing variant is at least (1-1/k)d off the optimum on negatively correlated demands; dot-product / L2 "geometric" packing is a few percent (up to ~10 percent) better, still within a few percent of optimum. quoted, txt 249-275, 19-23, 497-500.
- P3 (Christensen-Khan-Pokutta-Tetali survey 2017, section 4 read): online first fit for vector packing is d+7/10 competitive (Garey et al. 1976 restated); online lower bound d^(1-eps) (Azar et al.); online vector scheduling O(log d/log log d) and that is tight. quoted, txt 1286-1290, 1331-1335.
- P4 (Sandeep 2021, intro read): no better than a (log d) factor for vector bin packing when d is a large constant; Woeginger's 1997 2-D APX-hardness (proof corrected by Ray) is restated. quoted, txt 71-96.
- P5 (Das-Sun 2025, read in main parts): ONLINE DAG with one or more scarce resources: any greedy (never-idle) algorithm can be Omega(n)-competitive; no online algorithm beats (d-1)/2 for d>=3 resource types; a deterministic O(d + min{log |V|, log t_max}) algorithm exists. quoted (theorem forms verified in the HTML version), txt 188-193, 398-410.
- P6 (Perotin-Sun-Raghavan 2021, main parts): restates Garey-Graham 1975: m-approx (precedence, 1 resource), (d+1) for independent jobs when machines are not constraining, (d+2-(2d+1)/m) for any m; any list scheduler with only local priorities is no better than d-approximate. quoted, txt 140-146, 1138.
- P7 (Demirci-Hoffmann-Kim STACS 2018, intro and results): resource plus precedence plus machines of different speeds together: O(log m log n) for weighted completion time; identical machines 2+2log(n+1); Garey-Graham gives 3-3/m for one resource. quoted, txt 143-154, 185.
- P8 (Jansen-Rau 2021, abstract and intro): one scarce resource on m machines: NP-hard to beat 3/2, algorithm at 3/2+eps (earlier best 2+eps). quoted, txt 12-17, 60-65.
- P9 (Kovacs 2010 LPT on related machines, intro read): Gonzalez-Ibarra-Sahni 1977: LPT within 2m/(m+1) < 2 of optimum, and above 3/2 for some instances; best known now between 1.54 and 1+sqrt(3)/3=1.577. quoted, Algorithmica p. 414, txt 76-81.
- P10 (Berman-Charikar-Karpinski 2000, read): online, speeds known: slowest-fit is 2-competitive when the optimum load is known; not knowing it costs a factor up to 4 by doubling, 5.83 with a better rule; deterministic lower bound 2.438. quoted, txt 20-30, 116-125.
- P11 (Im-Kell-Panigrahi-Shadloo 2017, intro, theorems, appendix A named): vector jobs on machines of different speeds: if speed is the same in every resource, the ratio equals that of identical machines (O(log d/log log d)); if speed differs per resource, it equals unrelated machines (Omega(log d + log m)); slowest-fit fails for vector jobs. quoted, txt 156-165.

## Papers

### P1. C. Chekuri and S. Khanna, "On multidimensional packing problems", SIAM J. Comput. 33(4):837-851, 2004. Read from: https://chekuri.cs.illinois.edu/papers/vector-sicomp.pdf. Pages read: 837-847 and 851 in full (sections 1, 2.2, 3, conclusions); the PTAS machinery (2.1) and the PIP hardness proofs (4.1) skimmed only.
- Result: "Such problems naturally arise when scheduling tasks that have multiple resource requirements." (p. 837, txt 7-9). Vector scheduling: "to schedule n d-dimensional tasks on m machines such that the maximum load over all dimensions and all machines is minimized" (txt 3-5). The earlier best was naive: "the authors in [11] had to settle for a naive (d + 1)-approximation for the d-dimensional vector scheduling problem" (txt 90-91). This paper gives "a PTAS when d is fixed and an O(ln^2 d)-approximation when d is arbitrary" (txt 91-92). The naive rule: "treat them as one-dimensional vectors of size equal to the sum of their components ... apply the standard list scheduling algorithm of Graham" gives "a schedule of height at most V(J)/m + max p_j ... This yields a (d+1)-approximation" (Lemma 2.6, txt 476-487, p. 843). Simple randomised assignment: "an O(ln dm/ln ln dm)-approximation" (Theorem 2.15, txt 621). Lower bounds on the optimum used: the largest single component, and the volume per dimension divided by m (txt 460-470). Vector bin packing (as many bins as needed, each with capacity 1 in every resource): "(1 + eps*d + O(ln(1/eps)))-approximate" (Theorem 3.1, txt 643-645), so "for fixed d, VBP can be approximated to within O(ln d)" (Corollary 3.2, txt 686). Hardness: "unless NP = ZPP ... hard to approximate within a d^(1/2 - eps) factor" (Theorem 4.11, txt 895; the exponent is damaged in the text layer, given as d^(1/2-eps) in the table at txt 169-171 and in P3 txt 1214-1219) and "even when d = 2 the problem is APX-hard [31]; this is an interesting departure from the classical bin packing problem (d = 1) which exhibits an asymptotic FPTAS" (txt 150-153).
- Conditions: all demands known at the start (offline); each job is a vector of loads, loads add; the machines are identical; the goal is the worst load over every resource and machine (makespan-like), or the fewest bins.
- Measured effect: no experiments. Table 1.1 (txt 187-205): vector scheduling is (d+1) folklore, PTAS for fixed d, O(ln^2 d) for arbitrary d; vector bin packing is (d+eps) before, (1+eps*d+O(ln 1/eps)) now.
- Needs to know or measure: the full demand vector of every job on every resource (all d), and the number of machines.
- Failure modes: for arbitrary d "it is hard to approximate the VS problem to within any constant factor" (txt 143-144). "large gaps remain between the upper and lower bounds for both VS and VBP when the number of dimensions is arbitrary" (txt 902-905, p. 851).
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): the d resources are the scarce ones (cores, memory, a serial test slot, tokens). Summing the load across resources and list-scheduling costs a factor up to d+1 in the worst case; balancing per resource costs far less. This needs the per-job demand on every resource to be known before the job runs.

### P2. R. Panigrahy, K. Talwar, L. Uyeda, U. Wieder, "Heuristics for Vector Bin Packing", Microsoft Research technical report, 2011. Read from: https://www.labri.fr/perso/eyraud/pmwiki/uploads/Main/Panigrahy2011-VBPHeuristics.pdf (a mirror; the Microsoft page returned HTML). Pages read: all (about 16 pages, 789 text lines).
- Result: "any greedy algorithm has an approximation ratio of at most 2d" (txt 232). "For any integer k and large enough n there is an instance for which FFD partitions the n items into n/k bins, while it is possible to partition the items into (k-1)d n ... Thus the approximation ratio of FFD is at least (1 - 1/k)d" (Theorem 1, txt 249-253; the fraction layout is damaged, the final ratio is as printed). "even if the demand vector of each item is perturbed by O(1/dk^2), the approximation ratio of FFD is still Omega(d)" (txt 270-272). "we expect FFD not to perform particularly well when the input exhibits strong negative dependence across dimensions" (txt 275-276). "If the placement problem is effectively constrained by a single resource ... the problem is in essence one dimensional and FFD should probably be the algorithm of choice" (txt 180-182). The new heuristics pick, for the open bin, the item that maximises the weighted dot product with the bin's remaining capacity ("Dot-Product") or minimises the weighted L2 distance to it (txt 330-345). "We conclude that the new geometric heuristics L2 and DotProduct nearly always do as well as, and often noticeably outperform FFD-based heuristics" (txt 497-499).
- Conditions: static, offline; all items and bin capacities known; synthetic demand distributions in 9 classes (independent, positively and negatively correlated); n from 100 to 5000, d in {2,3,4,6,10}; 100 instances per class; compared against a configuration-LP lower bound (n up to about 100) or optima from Caprara and Toth (2-D, n=200); C#, a 3.16 GHz dual-core desktop.
- Measured effect: "can sometimes reduce the number of bins used by up to ten percent" (abstract, txt 19-20); "in all cases where we were able to compute the optimal solution we found our new heuristics within few percent of optimal" (txt 21-23); "For input classes 1, 4, and 5, where the average number of items per bin is large, ... the geometric heuristics outperform FFD by nearly 5 percent in dimension 6" (txt 489-492); bubble search (200 runs): saving about 0.25 percent on average (Table 1, txt 531-545) at about 200 times the time. Running time, 1000 items, 6 dimensions: FFDSum 0.0027 s, DotProduct 0.025 s, L2 0.024 s, bubble search 5.0 s (Table 2, txt 582). "the benefit is small when the dimensions are positively correlated (in which case they all reduce to the one dimensional case), the benefit increases when the dimensions are negatively correlated" (txt 599-602).
- Needs to know or measure: the full demand vector of every item, the capacity vector, and the dimension weights (the paper used a_i = exp(0.01 x average demand in dimension i), so the scarcer a resource is on average, the more it counts).
- Failure modes: "FFD-based heuristics may fail to take advantage of such complementarities" (a CPU-heavy item with an I/O-heavy one) (txt 288-290). The new heuristics are as bad as FFD on the paper's constructed instance (txt 363-370). The LP methods "do not scale beyond rather smallish instances" (txt 62-64). Synthetic data only: "it would be difficult to generalize from any given set of real workloads" (txt 404-406).
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): matters only if several resources bind at once and jobs differ in which one they use (a memory-heavy job next to a processor-heavy job). With one binding resource, plain size-ordered first fit is enough, which the authors say themselves. Needs demand estimates per resource before placement; a wrong estimate was not tested here.

### P3. H. I. Christensen, A. Khan, S. Pokutta, P. Tetali, "Multidimensional Bin Packing and Other Related Problems: A Survey", 2016 preprint (Computer Science Review 2017). Read from: https://tetali.math.gatech.edu/PUBLIS/CKPT.pdf. Pages read: section 4 (vector bin packing, online, vector scheduling, bin covering, heuristics; pp. 21-24) in full; the geometric sections 1-3 not read.
- Result: "The first paper to obtain an APTAS for 1-D bin packing ... implies a (d + eps) approximation for vector packing problem. Woeginger [Woe97] showed that there exists no APTAS even for d = 2 unless P = NP." (txt 1200-1202). Offline best: Bansal-Caprara-Sviridenko "(1 + ln d)", then Bansal-Elias-Khan "(1 + ln(1.5) + eps) (about 1.405+eps) for 2-D vector packing and a ln d + 0.807 + o_d(1) + eps-approximation" (txt 1213-1216, 1232-1236). Online: "A generalization of the First Fit algorithm by Garey et al. [GGJ76] gives d + 7/10 competitive ratio for the online version." (txt 1286-1287) and "Azar et al. [ACKS13] settled the status by giving (d^(1-eps)) information theoretic lower bound" (txt 1294-1296; exponent damaged in the layout, as printed in Table 4 and in P4 txt 77). Vector scheduling online: "Im et al. [IKKP14] ... O(log d/log log d)-competitive ratio. They also show tight information theoretic lower bound of Omega(log d/log log d)." (txt 1332-1335). A construction that shows the d^(1-eps) hardness for arbitrary d: one dimension per vertex of a graph; items fit together in a bin iff they form an independent set (txt 1206-1213).
- Conditions: survey, so each result keeps its own conditions; vector packing = d resources, all must stay within capacity in every bin; online = items arrive one by one and cannot be moved.
- Measured effect: none (survey of bounds). "Table 4: Present state of the art for vector packing and related variants" (txt 1349-1358).
- Needs to know or measure: item demand vectors; for online results, only the arriving item's vector.
- Failure modes: the paper states for online packing that the gap between 2 and d stayed open for a long time and was settled at d^(1-eps) by Azar et al. (txt 1291-1296): online vector packing can be nearly as bad as d times the optimum in the worst case.
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): the worst-case numbers grow linearly in the count of independent scarce resources (d+7/10 online first fit). With 2 to 4 scarce resources this is a small constant; the proofs do not say how typical agent workloads behave, only that the worst case is linear in d. The Garey et al. theorem itself was not read; this is its restatement by the survey (summary level for the original).

### P4. S. Sandeep, "Almost Optimal Inapproximability of Multidimensional Packing Problems", arXiv 2101.02854 (v2, June 2021; FOCS 2021). Read from: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2101.02854. Pages read: abstract and introduction (txt 1-130); proofs not read.
- Result: "We show that Vector Bin Packing has no Omega(log d) factor asymptotic approximation algorithm when d is a large constant, assuming P != NP. This matches the ln d + O(1) factor approximation algorithms (Chekuri, Khanna SICOMP 2004, Bansal, Caprara, Sviridenko SICOMP 2009, Bansal, Elias, Khan SODA 2016) upto constants." (abstract, txt 12-16). "Woeginger [Woe97] proved that there is no asymptotic PTAS for 2-dimensional Vector Bin Packing, assuming P = NP" (txt 71-72); footnote: "Very recently, Ray [Ray21] found an oversight in Woeginger's original proof and gave a revised APX hardness proof" (txt 96-97). Vector scheduling: "no polynomial time algorithm with an approximation ratio of Omega((log d)^(1-eps)) when d is part of the input" (abstract, txt 17-19), "This almost matches the O(log d/log log d) factor algorithms (Harris, Srinivasan JACM 2019, Im, Kell, Kulkarni, Panigrahi SICOMP 2019)" (txt 19-20).
- Conditions: offline, worst case, complexity assumptions as stated.
- Measured effect: none.
- Needs to know or measure: not applicable.
- Failure modes: Woeginger's 1997 hardness proof had a gap, since repaired (the quote above); I did not read Woeginger's 1997 paper (see "Could not open").
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): sets the limit of what any packing rule can promise with several resources: constant-factor guarantees do not exist for arbitrary d, and for fixed d the best known factor grows like ln d. Gives no guidance about the small d of one machine.

### P5. R. Das and H. Sun, "Almost Tight Approximation Hardness and Online Algorithms for Resource Scheduling", arXiv 2509.01086 (Sept 2025; SPAA 2025 version cited there). Read from: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.01086 plus the arXiv HTML version of the same paper (to recover math symbols). Pages read: introduction, results, related work, sections 5.2 (theorem), 6 (algorithm), 7, and conclusions; the lower-bound proofs in sections 3 and 5.1 skimmed.
- Result: Model: "jobs are unknown beforehand, and a job is revealed after all of its predecessor jobs have finished" (txt 96-97); each job needs units of one or more reusable resources "at all times during its execution". Theorem 6.1: "there is a deterministic online algorithm with competitive ratio O(d + min{log |V|, log t_max})" (txt 190-192; formula verified in the HTML text), with d resource types, |V| jobs, t_max the longest job. Theorem 5.6: "Let d >= 3 be the number of resource types. Then, no algorithm (even randomized ones) for online multi-resource scheduling can achieve a competitive ratio better than (d-1)/2." (txt 188-190; formula verified in the HTML text). Theorem 5.1: even with one resource, "no randomized online algorithm ... can be better than Omega(log n)-competitive or Omega(log t_max)-competitive" (txt 181-184). On a never-idle rule: "We call an algorithm greedy if whenever adequate resources are available in any point in time, it schedules job(s) from the set of jobs that are ready to execute. However, we show that every greedy algorithm could be as bad as Omega(n)-competitive, where n is the number of jobs (see Lemma 7.1). The problem arises due to the existence of long and skinny jobs (these jobs use significantly fewer resources but run for a long time) along with short and fat jobs (these jobs use all the resources but run for a very short time)." (txt 400-407; Omega(n) checked in the HTML text, line 575 there). Offline: "Garey and Graham [34] present a (3 - 3/m)-approximation algorithm. The result was later improved by Niemeier and Wiese [56] and, finally, an AFPTAS is known due to Jansen et al." (txt 466-469; fractions damaged in the layout, the 3-3/m reading agrees with P7 txt 151).
- Conditions: precedence DAG; a job becomes visible only when its predecessors finish; a job's length is known when it is revealed (the algorithm assigns levels from job lengths, txt 1893-1905); non-preemptive; resource amounts fixed per job; one budget per resource type.
- Measured effect: none (theory). The Omega(n) greedy instance (Fig. 5, txt 2214-2256): a long thin job started early blocks a later fat job; "any greedy algorithm's makespan is Omega(n^2)" against an optimum of Omega(n) (txt 2255-2258; exponents partly damaged in the layout).
- Needs to know or measure: for the O(d + min{log |V|, log t_max}) algorithm, job lengths on arrival, resource demand per job; it delays some ready jobs on purpose (it runs jobs "in stages" by level, txt 1900-1910) rather than starting everything that fits.
- Failure modes: stated above. "To the best of our knowledge, there is no non-trivial hardness of approximation ratio known for the offline setting when the number of resource types is a fixed constant" before this paper (txt 631-634).
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): this is the closest published model to a pipeline of agent jobs whose follow-up jobs appear only when the earlier ones finish. It says two things: a never-idle rule can lose a factor growing with the number of jobs when long, light jobs are started before short, heavy ones (a long agent session started before a short serial test run), and no online rule can beat a ratio that grows with the number of scarce resource types. It holds only if job length is known when the job is revealed, which for an agent is an estimate.

### P6. L. Perotin, H. Sun, P. Raghavan, "Multi-Resource List Scheduling of Moldable Parallel Jobs under Precedence Constraints", arXiv 2106.07059 (ICPP 2021). Read from: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2106.07059. Pages read: abstract, introduction, related work (section 2), model (3.1), theorem list, section 6 and conclusion (about 40 percent of 16 pages); the analysis of sections 4-5 not read.
- Result (restating Garey-Graham 1975, SIAM J. Comput. 4:187-200; the original was not read): "Garey and Graham [16] considered scheduling n sequential jobs on m identical machines with d additional types of resources. ... They presented a list-scheduling algorithm and proved three results: (1) an m-approximation for jobs with precedence constraints and when there is only one type of resource, i.e., d = 1; (2) a (d + 1)-approximation for independent jobs and when the number of machines is not a constraining factor, i.e., m >= n; (3) a (d + 2 - (2d+1)/m)-approximation for independent jobs with any m >= 2." (txt 140-146; in the text layer the last bound prints as "(d + 2 - 2dm+1 )", the fraction is damaged. My check: at d=1 the formula gives 3-3/m, which P7 (txt 151) and P5 (txt 466) both attribute to Garey-Graham for one resource: reasoned, consistent.) This paper's own result: "an approximation ratio of 1.619d + 2.545 sqrt(d) + 1 for any d, and a ratio of d + O(d^(2/3)) for large d" (abstract, txt 14-15; exponents damaged, as printed). "a lower bound of d on the approximation ratio of any list scheduling scheme with local priority considerations" (txt 16-17; Theorem 6, txt 1138-1139): "Any deterministic list scheduling algorithm with local job priority considerations is no better than d-approximation". Moldable-job context: "list scheduling achieves (d+1)-approximation" for malleable DAG jobs, from He et al. (txt 151-152, restated).
- Conditions: n jobs, d resource types with integer totals, job time depends on the allocation (moldable) and is known; precedence DAG; offline. The lower-bound instance (Fig. 2, txt 1146-1160): a tree of unit jobs, each needing one unit of one resource type, 2 units of each type available; "Any deterministic list scheduling algorithm with only local priority considerations cannot distinguish jobs that require the same resource type. Hence, in the worst case, it could only utilize one type of resource at any time" (txt 1158-1161), giving ratio about d.
- Measured effect: none (theory; the paper plots the estimated ratio against an actual ratio from its algorithm in Figure 1).
- Needs to know or measure: each job's per-resource demand and time; for a smarter rule, the DAG shape: "the lower bound ... does not rule out the possibility of a global list scheduling algorithm that considers the structure of the precedence graph when determining the priorities (e.g., giving priority to the jobs on the critical path)" (txt 1185-1189). That was left open.
- Failure modes: priorities that look only at one job at a time lose a factor of d; the m-approximation for one resource with precedence (Garey-Graham item 1) says that when the machine count is not the bottleneck, the bound stays large.
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): with d=2 or 3 binding resources the worst-case factors for plain list scheduling are small constants (3 or 4, from d+1), so a priority rule that sees the DAG (critical path first) is where the open theory says gains might be; no published bound confirms it.

### P7. G. Demirci, H. Hoffmann, D. H. K. Kim, "Approximation Algorithms for Scheduling with Resource and Precedence Constraints", STACS 2018 (LIPIcs 96, article 25). Read from: https://drops.dagstuhl.de/storage/00lipics/lipics-vol096-stacs2018/LIPIcs.STACS.2018.25/LIPIcs.STACS.2018.25.pdf. Pages read: abstract, introduction, related work, results (25:1-25:5); algorithm sections not read.
- Result: "We study non-preemptive scheduling problems on identical parallel machines and uniformly related machines under both resource constraints and general precedence constraints" (txt 20-22). "Theorem 1. There is a 2 + 2 log(n + 1)-approximation algorithm for P|res1, prec|Cmax." (txt 185); "an O(log m log n)-approximation algorithm for scheduling under these constraints on uniformly related machines" (abstract, txt 28-29); "This is the first upper bound on the approximability of this class of scheduling problems where both resource and general precedence constraints must be satisfied simultaneously." (txt 31-33). Without the combination: "(2 + eps)- and (2 - 1/m)-approximation algorithms, respectively" for resource-only and precedence-only (txt 54-55); "Garey and Graham's result in [5] implies a (3 - 3/m)-approximation for P|res1|Cmax. - Measured effect: none (theory).

- Failure modes: the bounds are logarithmic in n; "the approximability of the natural problem with the combination of these constraints is still wide open" before this work (txt 55-57).
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): shows how the resource and DAG constraints compose in the literature (separately handled, then stitched); gives a logarithmic, not constant, guarantee when both bind.

### P8. K. Jansen and M. Rau, "Closing the gap for single resource constraint scheduling", arXiv 2107.01613 (ESA 2022). Read from: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2107.01613. Pages read: abstract and introduction (pp. 1-3); algorithm sections not read.
- Result: "we are given m identical machines and a set of jobs, each needing one machine to be processed as well as a share of a limited renewable resource R ... It is NP-hard to approximate this problem with a ratio better than 3/2. On the other hand, the best algorithm so far has an absolute approximation ratio of 2 + eps. In this paper, we present an algorithm with absolute approximation ratio (3/2 + eps)" (txt 12-17). Also an APTAS "(1 + eps)OPT + p_max" (txt 63-66).
- Conditions: offline, durations and resource shares known, non-preemptive, one scarce resource besides machines.
- Measured effect: none.
- Needs to know or measure: each job's duration and resource share.
- Failure modes: 3/2 cannot be beaten in polynomial time unless P = NP (bin packing is a special case).
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): one serial resource (a test slot, a token budget) plus the processor cores is exactly this model. The theory shows that with known durations a 1.5 guarantee is the floor for any poly-time rule; simple rules sit around 3 (Garey-Graham, from P5/P7).

### P9. A. Kovacs, "New Approximation Bounds for LPT Scheduling", Algorithmica 57:413-433, 2010. Read from: https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00453-008-9224-9.pdf (open PDF). Pages read: abstract, introduction, start of section 2 (pp. 413-416); the proofs not read.
- Result (restating Gonzalez, Ibarra, Sahni, SIAM J. Comput. 6(1):155-166, 1977; the original was not read): "For different machine speeds, LPT was defined by Gonzalez, Ibarra, and Sahni [7]. They showed that in this general case Lpt/Opt <= 2m/(m+1) < 2, whereas for any eps > 0 an instance I exists so that Lpt(I)/Opt(I) > 3/2 - eps." (p. 414, txt 76-79; the fraction 2m/(m+1) is damaged in the text layer and is read as printed in "2m < 2, m+1"). "This result was later improved by Dobson [3], who obtained lower and upper bounds of 1.512 <= Lpt/Opt <= 19/12, and independently by Friesen [5] whose new bounds were 1.52 <= Lpt/Opt <= 5/3" (txt 79-82). This paper: "We tighten the upper bound to 1 + sqrt(3)/3 = 1.5773, and the lower bound to 1.54" (abstract, txt 17-19). LPT is defined as: jobs in non-increasing size, each to the machine "where it will be completed earliest" (Definition 1, txt 57-60). For identical machines Graham's exact ratio is 4/3 - 1/(3m) (txt 69-70).
- Conditions: offline; speeds s_i and job sizes known; job time = size/speed; no precedence, no resource constraint; makespan.
- Measured effect: none (theory); worst-case ratios above.
- Needs to know or measure: all job sizes and machine speeds, in advance.
- Failure modes: bound is worst-case and not tight (gap 1.54 to 1.577).
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): relevant only when workers have unequal speeds (for instance a fast pool and a slow pool of agent slots, or a model that is two times quicker). Largest-first with earliest completion time loses at most about 1.58 of the optimum for pure sizes; it is not known to carry over once a serial resource also binds.

### P10. P. Berman, M. Charikar, M. Karpinski, "On-line Load Balancing for Related Machines", J. Algorithms 35(1):108-121, 2000 (revised technical report version of 1999). Read from: https://theory.cs.uni-bonn.de/ftp/reports/cs-reports/1999/85210-CS.pdf. Pages read: abstract, introduction and section 2.1 (pp. 1-3); the analysis and lower-bound proofs not read.
- Result: "We consider the problem of scheduling permanent jobs on related machines in an on-line fashion. We design a new algorithm that achieves the competitive ratio of 3 + sqrt(8) = 5.828 for the deterministic version, and 3.31/ln 2.155 = 4.311 for its randomized variant, improving the previous competitive ratios of 8 and 2e = 5.436. We also prove lower bounds of 2.4380 on the competitive ratio of deterministic algorithms and 1.8372 on the competitive ratio of randomized algorithms" (abstract, txt 20-30; square roots are damaged in the layout and read from the numbers printed). "There exists a simple algorithm that achieves competitive ratio 2 if we know exactly the optimum load: we simply assign each job to the slowest machine that would not increase its load above 2 x optimum. Because we do not know it, we make a safely small initial guess and later double it whenever we cannot schedule a job within the current load threshold. In the worst case, the final guess is almost twice the optimum, and thus the load created by the jobs scheduled in that phase can be almost four times the optimum; ... jobs scheduled in the previous phases can add load of the same magnitude. ... converting from known to unknown increases the resulting load at most four times." (txt 116-125, p. 2-3; shortened between the ellipses).
- Conditions: online; machine speeds known; job sizes revealed on arrival and each job is placed at once and never moved; objective is the maximum machine load (jobs are permanent: no durations, no departures).
- Measured effect: none (theory).
- Needs to know or measure: speeds of all machines in advance; for the factor-2 rule, the optimum load (or a guess that is doubled when proven too small).
- Failure modes: not knowing the optimum costs a factor between 2 and 5.83 in the worst case; deterministic lower bound 2.438 means no online rule reaches 2.
- On one machine with agents (reasoned): when the job stream is unknown, "put the job on the slowest worker that keeps its load under a doubled guess of the optimum" is the published rule, with ratio about 6 worst case; with an estimate of total work the ratio is 2.

- Measured effect: none (theory).

- Failure modes: the cheap rule that works for scalar jobs (slowest-fit) fails once jobs have several resources (txt 88-89, appendix A).
