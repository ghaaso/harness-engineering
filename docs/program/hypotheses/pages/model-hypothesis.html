The model: two designs to build, train on the cards and compare

Nothing was built, trained or downloaded for this. It is the argued plan for the two models, their training on the three rented cards and the comparison between them. It stands on the data hypothesis, which you have not passed yet.

The answer in short

  1. Two models are proposed, to be trained and compared side by side. Both start from the same groundwork: for every file alive at the moment of the question, everything that can be counted exactly is computed, relative to the question and on the repository's own scale. They differ in what learns on top of it.
  2. Model Record, trees, reader: trees plus a reader. Boosted trees read the exact record and rank every file. Record, trees, reader tuned language model then reads the question and each of the top files together, with the record beside it, and corrects the top of the list. Each stage is trained on what the stage before it gets wrong.
  3. Model Graph rooted at the question: a graph rooted at the question. Record, trees, reader linear stage over the same exact columns ranks every file. For the top files a small network passes sums along typed, directed links from the question's own files, weighing each earlier change by what it was about; the top sixteen also get a joint reading of question and file.
  4. Training. Everything exact is built before the cards are rented, and the leak tests run on it then. On the cards three queues run that never wait for each other: one trains model Record, trees, reader whole, one model Graph rooted at the question whole, the third the arms of the comparison.
  5. The comparison. Record, trees, reader floor of untrained baselines on the same questions; each model whole; from each a ladder of arms that each remove one decision; one bridge arm that puts B's graph on A's first stage, which is the single decision that separates the two. Every arm carries a prediction, named before the run, that can fail.
  6. What this is not. Nothing here is measured. On the yardstick both models are met by construction on three profiles and open on four, and every open point waits on a count or a test that section 6 names. The search organised the reasoning and made it inspectable; it does not prove a design right.

1What is computed exactly for every file, before anything is learned

The options weighed

  • (a)Record, trees, reader typed graph with frozen text on its nodes, everything else learned on it.not chosen
  • (b)Record, trees, reader tuned code model reads the text, while amounts go round it on an exact side path.not chosen
  • (c)Exact sums over typed paths, exact name and text matches, and a learned ranker that reads them. Six further options were the same three mechanisms with more points unmet.chosen

The choice

(c) as the groundwork of both models. For every file alive at the asking time: the decayed, size-weighted counts of earlier joint changes; counts of typed links over one and two steps; reach along any path; an echo through similar earlier units of work; exact matches of names, paths and words; the best similarity of chunk vectors from one frozen encoder. Every amount enters as a logarithm, as its rank within the repository or the question, and with a flag where it is missing (Data 5.3). No file and no repository has an identity (Model 9.10). Beside these, each file carries a description of its place in the code graph that does not depend on the question: the return probabilities of a random walk, and its degree per kind of link.

Why

Record, trees, reader function the model is handed cannot be lost while learning, and the test of the yardstick is exactly such a function: the decayed, size-weighted count (Model 9.5). In our own record the count features alone scored the same as a graph network built on them. Amounts on the repository's own scale are what lets coefficients learned on one repository be read on another (Model 1.2, Model 7.1, Model 8.1); a tree cannot extrapolate past the range it saw, which relative amounts avoid (Data 8.1). Record, trees, reader hand-built table loses whatever it did not foresee (Data 9.2, Data 4.3); model Graph rooted at the question, which keeps events as nodes with nothing added up in advance (Data 9.3), is there to test that. For a file's place in the graph no description is best on every graph (Model 10.1); return probabilities are compact, the same under relabelling and free of arbitrary signs, and their one limit, that two files can share a signature (Model 10.2, Model 10.4), is what the marks of decision 2 answer.

Why the others lost

As the only form, (a) leaves every count to learned sums over sampled neighbours (Model 14.2), and (b) leaves it to a reader that reads digits badly and is not built to count (Model 13.9). Record, trees, reader learned vector per file or per repository cannot say anything about a file it has no row for (Model 13.7).

2How the question enters, and how a candidate is described relative to it

The options weighed

  • (a)The question is a node; its own files are marked by kind (in the change, named in the text, in the trace); a propagation starts from them.chosen
  • (b)An exact record per pair of question and candidate, over the full list of typed paths, plus one joint reading of both texts.chosen
  • (c)One reader takes question and candidate as one sequence, with a token per kind of relation: attention alone knows no graph, and tokens are one of the three places where a graph can enter it (Model 12.6, Model 12.7).chosen

The choice

(b) in model Record, trees, reader and (a) in model Graph rooted at the question; the joint reading of (c) acts inside both, on the top files only. The marks are relative identities, so no table of files is learned (Model 9.10), and the question's own files are an anchor set that every question draws afresh (Model 10.5). The score is read from the candidate's own vector by a fixed, shallow read-out (Model 5.4).

Why

The earlier graph models described each file once, for all questions, and one of them ended up ranking by how often a file changes at all. Marks and pair records make the description depend on the question by construction. Reading two texts together costs one pass per pair, so it is spent on a shortlist (Model 12.1).

Why the others lost

Record, trees, reader score made only from two vectors, each computed without the other: two candidates with the same surroundings then get the same score, whatever the question (Model 9.4, Model 9.8); and a position task cannot be done from structure alone (Model 9.7). Positions from eigenvectors with arbitrary signs (Model 10.3, Model 10.6). Record, trees, reader random identity per file, averaged over draws, would also break the symmetry and still transfer (Model 10.7); it is not taken, because the question's own marks do the same and cost nothing.

3History: from the events that link two files to what the scorer sees

The options weighed

  • (a)Record, trees, reader fixed bank of exact sums: several size weights, several decay lengths, on two clocks of the repository's own, with the order of the two changes kept.chosen
  • (b)Record, trees, reader learned sum over event nodes, with the exact count kept as a fixed channel.not chosen
  • (c)Record, trees, reader bounded gate per event that reads the question and what the event was about; with the gate at rest the sum is the exact count.chosen

The choice

(a) for every file, in both models. On top of it events are weighed by what they were: in model Record, trees, reader the bank is also summed apart by what was done to the candidate (edited, added, renamed, deleted) and by bulk and bot flags, and the reader's head reads the gate of (c); in model Graph rooted at the question the gate of (c) sits on the event edges of the propagation. Neighbours treated differently is what an edge gate adds over one kernel for all (Model 9.3).

Why

Record, trees, reader mass rename and a six-file feature of the same age weigh the same in any kernel; only something that reads the event can tell them apart: the fact's own content has to be in its message (Model 13.9). Record, trees, reader fixed bank alone loses what it did not foresee; events kept as they are lose nothing (Data 9.2, Data 9.3). Record, trees, reader gate that is bounded and per event, not a softmax over events, still counts.

Why the others lost

Record, trees, reader mean, a maximum or a normalised attention as the only aggregation: none of them can count (Model 9.5, Model 12.6, Model 13.2). Record, trees, reader fixed window of the most recent events as the only view: counting restricted to the same window scored below counting over all history (Data 2.10).

Trees over the bank do no better per repository than the best single kernel; or the gate stays at rest on validation repositories, or gains on training repositories only.

5Time

The options weighed

  • (b)Relative clocks only, and a rule for any pretrained part: its training cut-off must come before the validation stretch, and every example is marked by it.chosen

All three, each for what it alone does: (a) keeps later facts out by construction and gives the exact bank; (c) gives each unseen repository its own curve, shapes that are not monotone included; (b) keeps what a pretrained model has read out of every choice made on validation.

6Reach to files that history does not connect

The options weighed

  • (a)Exact reach columns for every file: restart walks from the question's files over each group of links, with the walk's leaning towards well-connected files removed; distance in links to those files (Model 10.5); the echo through similar earlier work; content matches.chosen
  • (b)Record, trees, reader probability for every file by walking down the folder tree, the repository's own hierarchy (Model 12.4).not chosen
  • (c)Channels of a language model: similarity of vectors, and names the model proposes that must resolve in the repository's own table of symbols.not chosen

The choice

(a) in both models. One score ranks every file alive; the top files are read jointly, each over a direct edge from the question, which is what removes the long route (Model 11.3, Model 12.5).

Why

By repository, 30, 66 and 21 % of right files had never changed with the question's file before. Record, trees, reader walk computed once has no depth limit and nothing to learn (Model 2.3, Model 14.4): it is a linear filter whose coefficients do not depend on the graph, so it runs on any repository unchanged (Model 3.1), on the degree-normalised form of the graph, a choice that matters where degrees differ a lot (Model 2.1); it favours well-connected files by a known amount, which is divided out (Data 2.8, Data 2.9). The four combined designs split on this decision: the two that took (c) had no channel for a file tied only by a longer route or by similar earlier work.

Why the others lost

Reach by stacking local steps: a network sees as far as its layers and no further, and what lies far is squeezed into one vector on the way (Model 5.5, Model 11.1, Model 11.2). Attention over all pairs of files of a repository: practical only when the number is small (Model 12.3). Depth and width beyond what transfer allows (Model 8.4).

7The score, the loss, the negatives, the weights

The options weighed

  • (a)The score is the untrained baseline of the kind of question plus a learned part; each right file is ranked against the wrong files only; every candidate with any relation to the question is taken whole and the rest are sampled with known probabilities and weighted back.chosen
  • (b)Trees with pairs weighted by how much a swap changes the measure.not chosen
  • (c)Record, trees, reader calibrated probability per file, which can also say how many files to show.not chosen

The choice

(a) for every trained stage of both models. Record, trees, reader new file is a slot per folder, a virtual entry of its own (Model 13.5). Every repository carries the same total weight, with a bound per example; steps of one unit of work share one weight.

Why

With the learned part at zero the score is the baseline itself, on the same examples (Data 7.2). No right file sits in another's denominator, so a change with five right files does not push four of them down. Fewer sampled negatives mean more variance, which is why the related candidates are taken whole (Model 14.2, Data 2.10). (c) is not taken: a loss on probability is not aimed at the order; what it would add, a stopping point, is given up for now.

Why the others lost

Negatives that are real answers: in our record, labelling earlier real pairs as false took a score from 0.141 to 0.007 (Model 13.6). Samples whose probabilities are not kept, or a sample treated as if it were the whole (Data 3.1): without them a total cannot be estimated (Data 3.2).

9The two models to build

The options weighed

  • (a)a graph spine;chosen
  • (b)a cascade under a large tuned code model;not chosen
  • (c)the exact record, trees and a joint reader;chosen
  • (d)a question-rooted graph over a tuned code model.not chosen

The choice

Model Record, trees, reader is (c) after its improvement pass, model Graph rooted at the question is (a) after its own. Record, trees, reader: exact record; trees with shallow trees per kind; in a shortlist a tuned reader whose head also reads the record and the question gate. 10Training on the three cards

The options weighed

  • (a)Each model as one job across all cards, with the exact columns recomputed on the card.not chosen
  • (b)Rows built once, stages trained apart, three jobs that never wait for each other.chosen
  • (c)One reader trained once and shared by both models.not chosen
  • (d)Two large readers teach a small one.not chosen
  • (e)Both models trained at the size the 12 GB card can run.not chosen
  • (f)Train large and round the weights.not chosen

The choice

(b). Before the card window: one pass in time per repository writes the rows of every question with both models' exact columns and the sampled negatives with their probabilities; the frozen encoder embeds each chunk once per content; the first stage of each model is fitted on all its rows, on scores from fits that did not see the example; shortlists are frozen; the baselines and first-stage arms are scored; the leak tests run on the stored rows (Data 6.4, Data 9.4). In the window: card 1 trains model Graph rooted at the question whole (about five and a half hours), then the bridge arm; card 3 trains model A's reader whole (about five hours); card 2 runs the rungs of the ladders on a quarter of the data; each card scores in its last hour. Every job saves its state every half hour, a watchdog restarts a silent job, each slot has a hard end and arms are dropped in a fixed order.

Why

Record, trees, reader computation that needs no learning is done once (Model 14.4). Independent jobs lose one card to a fault, not three. Files drawn one by one would arrive without their neighbours (Model 14.1), so questions are batched per repository. What is sampled is sampled by a design whose probabilities are kept (Model 14.2, Data 3.2, Data 2.10), and a subgraph sample that drops links between groups would bias the gradient (Model 14.3). Where memory is short the work goes slower; the sample is not shrunk.

Why the others lost

(e): it sizes the design by the PC's card, against the owner's standing word. (a) leaves under an hour for the arms and cannot include trees in a joint step.

11The comparison

The options weighed

  • (a)Two ladders down to one floor, with a bridge between the models.chosen
  • (b)Record, trees, reader fractional factorial over the decisions.not chosen
  • (c)Record, trees, reader two-by-two of the two separating decisions above a shared reader.not chosen
  • (d)One cascade with inputs knocked out at the head.not chosen
  • (e)Record, trees, reader shared trunk with one swap per arm.not chosen
  • (f)Each model as its own ladder of stages.not chosen

The choice

(a). The floor, on the same questions: counting with the one-over-size weight, recently changed, same folder, import neighbours, text search, named files, trace files, frozen similarity. First-stage arms on processors: linear against trees on the same columns; trees without one block of columns at a time; raw logarithms in place of relative amounts. Card arms on a quarter of the data, each beside its reference: model Graph rooted at the question without the marks, without the exact columns in the starting state, without the gate, with kinds merged, without the joint reading; model Record, trees, reader without the record in the reader's head; one double removal. The bridge: B's propagation on A's first stage and shortlist, against A's reader. Each arm has a prediction on a segment named before the run. Results are read per repository first, then averaged with the sampling weights, with intervals from resampling clusters (Data 3.2); the test is scored once and never used to choose (Data 6.1).

Why

If a model cannot beat the naive forms, something is wrong (Data 7.2); an average can hide that a model is good in one segment and bad in another (Data 7.3). One decision per arm is what lets a result be attributed.

Why the others lost

Record, trees, reader sweep over forms or over a library's settings in place of an argument (Model 1.1). (e) never trains either model whole. (c) and (d) no longer train the models as they stand.

It rests on

Model 1.1 Data 3.2 Data 6.1 Data 7.2 Data 7.3

It is wrong if

Record, trees, reader reference trained on a quarter differs from its whole model in the named segments, so the ladder does not speak for the model; or the bridge leads, or trails, on both of its segments alike.

12Sizes, and the form for the 12 GB card

The options weighed

  • (a)Sizes by habit or by reputation of a model.not chosen
  • (b)Sizes found by a sweep.not chosen
  • (c)Sizes computed from the compute and the counts.chosen

The choice

(c). The reader's size follows from the card-hours of its slot, the tokens per pair and the number of pairs: about 120 million parameters at 50,000 questions on one card for five hours, about 590 million at 10,000; the largest the count allows is taken. The frozen encoder's class (0.1 to 0.3 billion) follows from the counted tokens. Model B's learned part stays small, three layers at a width of at most 256, with sums along links only, so that its cost grows with files and links and not with their pairs (Model 12.2), where transfer can be expected. The small form: the untrained and first stages run on processors, the reader at half precision on the shortlist on the 12 GB card; a smaller reader is distilled on the PC only if the time per question is too long.

Why

The bounds for a network on a graph other than the one it was trained on grow with depth and width, and hold only inside one family of graphs (Model 7.3, Model 8.2, Model 8.3, Model 8.4), and a network inherits the stability of its sums once per layer (Model 7.2); a filter sharp enough to tell everything apart is the one that transfers worst (Model 3.2). Large first, then shrink: the large form is whatever the eight hours allow, and the shrinking is done afterwards, on the PC.

Why the others lost

(a) and (b): a larger class fits its training data better and that says nothing about unseen data (Model 1.1); deeper and wider graph networks transfer worse (Model 8.4); in our record retrievers of three sizes swung by repository, not by size.

Counts to make first, in this order

  1. The pilot of about 30 repositories of the data design: the number of clusters, and with it every size below.
  2. Distinct tokens of content and of events: the encoder's class and the days its pass takes.
  3. Rows per question by repository: the upload, the processor hours, the time of the tree fits.
  4. Questions per kind, the linked issues and the error tiers: the reader's size, and whether error segments can be read at all.
  5. Links of the largest repository: whether model Graph rooted at the question fits one card.
  6. The share of right files inside each shortlist on validation: whether a second stage can matter.
  7. Validation questions per named segment: which predictions can be read.
  8. Step time and memory in each job's first fifteen minutes.
  9. Scoring time per arm.
  10. Free disk and memory where the work before the window runs: on the PC, or on a rented machine without cards.
