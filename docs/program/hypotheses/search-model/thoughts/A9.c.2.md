# A9 option 2: Train at deployed size, replicate by halves
Lens: departs from large first, then shrink. The cards buy replicates and arms, not a teacher.

**Mechanism.** The reader is sized by the 12 GB form: 2·N_12·L·|K| = F_12·t_q. F_12 is the PC card's speed, timed. t_q is the time per question the baselines allow. Each design is trained from labels at N_12, end to end: C1's J through φ from the start, C3's s_2 on out-of-fold s_0+s_1. There is no teacher.
Replicates: each design is trained on half H1 and on half H2 and scored on the same validation repositories. The spread between the two fits gives training variance, which resampling clusters cannot show. C3 already has the halves.
Computed once, off the cards: the shared record. Vectors are made on the PC if the counts allow.
Cards (one job each; F timed first):
- h0.25–1.25: C3's trees and shortlists.
- h1.25–4.5: card 1, C3's s_2 on H1, then on H2. Card 2, C1 on H1. Card 3, C1 on H2.
- h4.5–7: A10's arms at their parent's schedule, then fits on all data.
- h7–7.5: scoring. h7.5–8: reserve.
Sampling: known probability per repository (data choice D11); C1's neighbours with known π. Saved: checkpoints every 30 min under a watchdog, and scores per (question, candidate).

**Needs from other parts.** From C1: J trainable through φ, about 3 card-hours per epoch, the largest graph resident. From C3: the halves and the shortlist rule. From A10: arms that differ in one decision and run at equal compute.

**Can.** M8 by construction: the arms, both designs and the baselines run on the same examples, and the intervals include training variance. Without that, a gap between C1 and C3 cannot be told from noise. C1 keeps its end-to-end joint pass. A cheap reader can read a wider shortlist at the same t_q (M6 in part).

**Why it departs.** The material does not show that size transfers. N 1.1: a larger class fits training better and says nothing of unseen data. N 8.4: wider transfers worse. Record: retrievers of 0.14, 0.6 and 7 B swung by repository, not by size; 22 sizes of one graph model scored the same. A teacher would spend about 40 % of the cards on that open bet.

**Cannot.** It cannot see a gain from size; nothing is larger than N_12. Its ceiling is what N_12 learns from few task-word and error labels. A fit on half the data overstates the variance of a fit on all of it. Nearest veto W12: the size is computed from t_q, not swept.

**Rests on.** N 1.1, N 8.4, N 14.2; D 3.2, D 6.2, D 7.3; record: FACTS-MODELS-SO-FAR.md. Assumption: t_q is taken from the baselines. Count first: F, F_12, pairs.

**Refuted if.** The spread between the H1 and H2 fits is negligible against the gap between C1 and C3. Or a reader several times N_12, trained later on the same split, leads on unseen repositories beyond the intervals.
