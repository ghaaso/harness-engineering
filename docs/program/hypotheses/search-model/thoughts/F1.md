# F1: Staged rows, three card queues, two ladders
Parents: C1.r1, C3.r1, A9.a.2, A10.a.1

## What is built

**C1.r1 (propagation).** Exact: every file alive gets a column vector x̃(Q,c): the decayed one-over-size bank, lag hazard, typed one- and two-hop counts, restarted walks per relation group, echo, BM25, names, maximum chunk cosine, all relative to the repository. Learned stage 1 is a linear map over x̃ that ranks every file; its top K get a question node Q with marked starting files (a_k) and pair edges φ. Three layers of typed, directed, sum-aggregated message passing run on shared bases (R = 28) with per-channel question gates κ on event edges. The top K_J also get a joint pass J: question, best chunk and past messages read as one sequence by the encoder with adapters. The score is s1 + [c ∈ K] s2 (C1.r1, A2 to A8).

**C3.r1 (pair record and reader).** Exact: a record R_ex(q,c) per candidate: typed path counts to two links (at most 468 types), the bank split by event kind, the per-repository lag curve H, reach and echo, text columns. Learned: s_1, gradient-boosted trees over R_ex with shallow per-kind residual trees; s_2, a pretrained code-and-text reader that reads question and candidate content as one input on a shortlist (top M by s_0 + s_1 united with the text channels' top K). Its head also reads R_ex and a question-gated event sum (C3.r1, A2, A8).

**What differs.** C1 learns aggregates over a sampled typed neighbourhood. C3 enumerates them exactly and learns trees over them, then spends its learned capacity on a joint text reading. This is the one decision the bridge arm isolates (A10.a.1).

**Built once, shared.** The loader that cuts the store at t_q (D6, D 9.4). The negative sample of A7.c.1 with π_c stored, and the offset b_k. One frozen code-and-text encoder for chunk and event vectors. The union of both designs' exact columns. The floor of baselines (D12).

## What is computed once and stored

1. **On the PC's processors, before rental** (A9.a.2). One time-ordered pass per training repository writes, per question, the rows R(q) whole plus the Poisson sample with π_c, carrying the union of x̃ and R_ex in 8-bit bins. The same pass writes the typed repository graphs C1 propagates over, cut per question at loading. Validation and test questions are written the same way. D13's cut-against-whole and planted-fact tests run on these stored rows before any card time (D 9.4, D 6.2).
2. **On the PC's 12 GB card** (changed, see below). The frozen encoder embeds each chunk once per content hash and each event message once per commit.
3. **On the PC's processors, out of fold** on two halves of the training clusters (A9.a.2): C1's linear s1 and C3's trees and per-kind residuals. The shortlists are then frozen: C1's K and K_J by s1, C3's M ∪ K. The token sequences of each shortlisted pair are written. All stage-1 arms and every floor baseline are scored here on the same questions.
4. **Sent to the rented machine:** the rows, graphs, vectors, token files, frozen shortlists and offsets of public repositories only. A9.a.2's estimate is about 100 GB of rows. Count first: pair token files (about 3×10^5 questions × 150 pairs × 512 tokens) and graph sizes.

## The plan for the three cards, hour by hour

Three queues that never synchronise (A9.a.2). Two hand-offs are by file only, each with hours of margin.

| Hours | Card 1 | Card 2 | Card 3 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 to 0.75 | C1 top: stage 2 with J trained, on all training questions | J cache: untuned encoder, quarter sample, both shortlists | C3 top: reader on all training questions |
| 0.75 to 2.25 | C1 top (continued) | C3-ref (quarter) | C3 top (continued) |
| 2.25 to 3.0 | C1 top (continued) | C1-ref (quarter, J frozen) | C3 top (continued) |
| 3.0 to 5.0 | C1 top (continued) | five C1 rungs, 0.75 each (to 6.75) | C3 top (ends at 5.0) |
| 5.0 to 6.5 | C1 top (ends at 5.5); bridge 5.5 to 6.25 | C1 rungs (continued) | C3 rung without R_ex (quarter) |
| 6.5 to 7.0 | double removal 6.25 to 7.0 | C1 rungs end 6.75; 0.25 buffer | C3 gate: head retrained on C3-ref's body |
| 7 to 8 | scoring: C1 top, bridge, double removal | scoring: C3-ref, C1-ref, five rungs | scoring: C3 top, two C3 arms |

**What is sampled, and by which design.** Repositories and questions are drawn as D11 says, with probabilities kept (D 3.2). The negatives are fixed in the rows. C1 samples neighbours in stage 2 with π_u on the exact edge weight and weights them 1/π_u (C1.r1 A4; D 2.9). Each quarter is one sample of training clusters drawn with known probability, shared by every rung and its reference (A10.a.1).

**Memory, argued.** C1: 1 to 4 GB per question graph, one repository resident at a time (C1.r1). Count first: the largest repository's edges must fit 96 GB. The joint pass is 16 × 512 tokens through a 0.1 to 0.3 B encoder with backward, which is small beside 96 GB. C3: N = C/(6LP) (C3.r1's own rule). With C = 5 card-hours × 3,600 × 1.5×10^14 (assumption, as in A9.a.2 and C3.r1) = 2.7×10^18, L = 512 and P = 7.5×10^6 pairs (50,000 questions × 150), N ≈ 1.2×10^8. At 10,000 questions N ≈ 5.9×10^8. Weights with optimiser state are about 16 bytes per parameter, under 10 GB, so the card holds it.

**Time, argued.** C1's own figures are under 1 h per epoch of stage 2 on three cards and about 1 h per epoch of the joint pass. That makes about 6 card-hours per pass on one card, so 5.5 h is about 0.9 of a pass. A quarter rung with J frozen and cached costs under 3/4 card-hour. The C3 quarter: 1.5 h is at least 5.0/4, so the reference sees the same pass at the same N. J cache: 7.5×10^4 quarter questions × 16 pairs × 512 tokens × 2 shortlists ≈ 1.2×10^9 tokens × 3×10^8 forward operations ≈ 0.7 h.

**Saved.** Every job checkpoints every 30 min to the machine's disk. A slot's checkpoint, final weights and per-question scores are copied back at its end. Per question, the scores kept are the ranks of the right files and the top 100. Teacher scores for the 12 GB student are computed later on the PC from the saved weights, so they cost no card time.

**If a step runs long.** Each slot has a hard end. A top that is cut stays at its last checkpoint. Its questions run in a random order of a weighted draw, so a partial pass is still a known-design sample. Arms are dropped in a fixed order: the double removal, then C3's gate head, then the C1 rung whose named segment has the fewest validation questions (count first). Scoring runs tops first, then the bridge, then rungs; an arm not scored by hour 8 is reported as not run. Added: a watchdog stops a job that logs no step for 10 min and resumes it from its checkpoint.

**Changed to fit.**
1. A9.a.2 gave the C3 reader two cards data-parallel and C1 one card of 6.5 h. Now each top has one card and card 2 holds the ladders. Reason: A10.a.1's arms need about 9 card-hours, and A9.a.2 cut them from the tops' own jobs. C1 gets 5.5 h (about 0.9 pass). C3 gets 5.0 h, and its N falls to match by its own rule, so it still makes one pass.
2. A9.a.2's measuring half-hour is now the first 15 min of each job, and those steps are kept. Reason: 24 card-hours have no slack.
3. Chunk embedding moved from processors to the PC's card. Reason: C1.r1's count is about 2×10^10 tokens × 2×10^8 operations, which is weeks on processors. On the PC's card it is about a day at an assumed 5×10^13 effective operations per second. Count first: tokens.
4. Stage-1 trees are fitted on a question sample with known π, sized to the PC's 31 GB (about 2×10^7 rows). It is then applied to all rows as a stream. Reason: C3.r1's 2.5×10^8 rows were sized for card memory, and A9.a.2 puts stage 1 before rental. The cut: s_1 learns from fewer rows. C1's linear s1 streams over all rows.
5. Added: checkpoints every 30 min and the watchdog. Teacher scores are computed on the PC.

## The comparison

**Floor, on the same questions** (D12; A10.a.1): b_k; counting with 1/n; recently changed; same folder; import neighbours; BM25; named files; trace files; frozen cosine.

**Stage-1 arms, on processors.** Each differs from the arm above it in one decision, and each carries a prediction that can fail:
- L (C1's linear over x̃) against T_x (trees over x̃): linear or trees. Prediction: T_x leads L only in the extreme size strata.
- T_x against T (trees over R_ex): the typed path grammar and the event-kind bank. Prediction: T leads on the segment "right file's link kind" (C3.r1).
- T without path types, without event kinds, without H, without reach and echo, without per-kind trees. Predictions: dropping reach and echo costs on right files with no joint history and no two-hop path (C1.r1 and C3.r1, Reach). Dropping H moves per-repository ranks beyond the intervals (Lag hazard).
- L with raw logs in place of quantiles. Prediction: it loses in the extreme size strata (C1.r1, Relative scale).

**Card arms.** Each rung is trained on the quarter, beside its reference:
- C1-ref without a_k. Prediction: rank correlation between questions asked at the same moment rises toward 1.
- Without U x̃. Prediction: it loses among right files with both content and history signal.
- Without the gate. Prediction: it loses on unseen repositories, not only on training ones.
- Types merged with a one-hot kind. Prediction: it loses on the segment "link kind".
- Without J. Prediction: it loses on right files with no history link.
- Double removal (U x̃ with the gate): the one redundancy test.
- C3-ref without R_ex in the head. Prediction: it loses where trace depth and message must combine.
- C3 with the gate head retrained: a per-repository move beyond the intervals.
- **Bridge:** C1's stage 2 on C3's out-of-fold offset and shortlist, against C3-ref: propagation or reader. Prediction, written before the run: the bridge leads on right files within two hops with joint history, and C3-ref leads on right files with no joint history and no code link. It fails if neither holds.
- Tops: each against the floor, then the two against each other as bundles.

**Reading.** Each arm is paired per repository with the rung above it. Results are averaged with 1/π weights, with intervals by resampling clusters inside strata (D12). A decision holds if removing it costs more than the interval in its segment, named before the run (D 7.3). Results are read by tier, by question kind, by history length and by "repository created after the encoder's cut-off". The test is scored once.

**Changed to fit.**
1. In the C1 ladder, J is frozen at the untuned encoder, not taken from the top. Reason: the top ends at 5.5 h, and the ladder cannot wait. The cost: top against ref now mixes J's tuning with data size.
2. C3's top is 5.0 h, not 5.5. Reason: room for the J cache and the gate head, which A10.a.1 did not price.
3. Stage-1 arms run on the row sample of change 4 above.
4. Scoring and drop orders are added for overruns.

## The smaller form for a 12 GB card

The pipeline stops on the PC. s_0 and stage 1 of either design run on processors. C3's reader at N ≤ 6×10^8 takes under 1.2 GB of weights in half precision and runs on the shortlist as it is (C3.r1). C1's stage 2 runs one question at a time, with only the top-K neighbourhood moved to the card (C1.r1). A student distilled from teacher scores computed on the PC is built only if inference time per question exceeds a bound set from his use (A9.a.2; count first). The owner's two repositories are scored only here.

## What must be counted before any run

1. The pilot of about 30 repositories (D2) gives the number of clusters, and with it every size below.
2. Distinct content-hash tokens and event tokens decide the encoder's size class and the days on the PC's card.
3. |R(q)| and rows per question by repository decide the tree sample, the upload size and the processor hours of the pass.
4. Questions per kind (linked issues, error tiers) give P, and so C3's N. They also decide whether the error segments can be read.
5. Edges of the largest repository decide whether C1 fits 96 GB, and fix its epoch time.
6. Recall at K, K_J and M on validation decides whether stage 2 can matter, and sets K.
7. Validation questions per named segment decide which predictions can be read, and the drop order.
8. Step time and memory in each job's first 15 min confirm the slots.
9. Scoring time per arm.

## What the owner is asked to allow

- **Downloads.** One frozen code-and-text encoder from a public model hub, of the 0.1 to 0.3 B class (under 1 GB), with a pretraining cut-off before the validation stretch. One pretrained code-and-text reader for C3, at the size computed from count 4 (10^8 to 6×10^8 parameters, 0.5 to 2.5 GB). The public repositories and the event archive are already asked for by the data hypothesis (D2).
- **Sent to the rented machine.** Derived rows, graphs, vectors and token files of public training, validation and test repositories (count first; on the order of 200 GB). The two pretrained models. The training code.
- **Never leaves the PC.** His two private repositories and the old held-out sets (D15, W13). They are scored on the PC with the weights brought back.

## Against the sheet

- **M1: by construction for C1; in part for C3** (outside the shortlist, the cosine channel for task words). Staging changes neither design's φ, a_k or record (N 9.4, N 9.8, N 9.10).
- **M2: by construction.** The decayed one-over-size count is stored exact in the rows and never sampled. Only candidates and C1's stage-2 neighbours are sampled, with 1/π (N 9.5; D 2.10).
- **M3: by construction for identities and scale; open on a test.** There are no identities, and amounts are quantiles. Open: repositories created after the encoder's cut-off (N 13.7; D 8.1).
- **M4: by construction.** There is one cut view, and the leak tests run on stored rows before card time. Open on a count: validation questions after the cut-off (D 9.4, D 6.2).
- **M5: by construction** in both designs (N 13.2, N 13.8).
- **M6: in part.** By construction for files reached by a path, word, name or echo. Open on recall at K and M, because staging freezes stage 1 (A9.a.2's Cannot).
- **M7: open on counts 2 to 6, 8 and 9.** Every arm has a slot. Memory and time are argued. Negatives have known π and are never answers. Weights follow D11 (N 14.2, N 13.6; D 3.2).
- **M8: by construction**, in part for redundancy. There is one decision per rung, the floor runs on the same questions, predictions are named per arm, and results are read per repository and per segment (D 7.2, D 7.3).

**Vetoes nearest.** W9: shortlists use out-of-fold offsets, every setting is chosen on validation, segments are named first, and the test is scored once. W12: every arm is a named decision, and N is computed, not swept. W13: private repositories stay on the PC, every download is named, and the plan fits 24 card-hours. W10: trees read only relative amounts. W1: the cosine channel is never the only score. W6: attention stays within one pair.

## Cannot

- Let stage 1 learn from stage 2. A right file ranked below K or M stays there in both designs.
- Average negative variance across epochs. Each top makes at most one pass, on one fixed sample.
- Test A9.a.2's own refutations (joint against staged C1, re-drawn negatives). There are no card-hours for them.
- Separate J's tuning from data size in C1 top against C1-ref.
- See redundancy beyond the one double removal. It cannot see a gain that shows only at full scale in a rung.
- Give C3's s_1 all its rows. It learns from a PC-sized sample.
- Read the error segments if count 7 is small. Their residuals then stay zero (C3.r1).
- Compare the two tops as more than bundles.

## Refuted if

- A quarter reference differs from its top in the named segments, so the ladder does not speak for the top (A10.a.1).
- The double removal loses beyond the interval while neither single removal does (A10.a.1).
- The bridge leads, or trails, C3-ref on both named segments alike, so the decision is not where the designs say.
- Recall at K or M on unseen repositories leaves the right files without joint history mostly outside the shortlist, so frozen stage 1 caps both designs.
- Neither top's lead over b_k exceeds its interval on unseen repositories after the cut-off, while it does before the cut-off.
- The stage-1 arms on the PC sample order differently from a larger sample drawn the same way, so the sample size decides the result.
