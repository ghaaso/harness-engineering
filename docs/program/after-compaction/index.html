One stretch of a session, from one compaction to the next. After is the 30 tool calls that follow a compaction. Before is the 30 calls that end where the next summary's input ended. The few calls the harness keeps word for word are in neither. The placebo is the same pair of windows around a point in the middle of a stretch, where nothing happened. No call sits in two windows.

Do the calls after a compaction go wrong more often?

How to read it. Each row is one count, for each 100 calls. The ring is the 30 calls before, the filled dot the 30 calls after. How to read it. Each row is one group of hand-overs. The dot is how much the count rises from before to after, the line its range, the vertical line is no change. Can the script's flags be trusted?

How to read it. The summary check's script marked 84 claims wrong and all 84 were its own mistakes. So every kind of flag here was read by hand: up to 15 events of each, picked by a hash, without knowing which window an event was in. The bar is the share that were the session's own mistakes. The ticks are the two lines fixed beforehand: 80 % to count as a mistake kind, 50 % to be kept at all.
