What works for the session monitor: the research

Round 6, stage A, 24 November 2026. Each picture is the tool's own, loaded from its source. Under each: what it does well for us, what would break with our data (8 sessions at once, about 800 helpers, 19 pipelines), and how I know it.

The answer, in five lines
  1. Few fixed boxes, thickness for amount. The process pictures that read at a glance keep about 10 to 15 boxes in fixed places and show how much went along each route by the thickness of the arrow. They do not draw each case (Disco, Minard). Our 800 helpers become thickness and counts, not lines.
  2. One line per session through time is the known failure. Round 5 was a storyline chart. That form works only while the lines rarely cross. Where they cross often, as in the xkcd "Primer" panel, it is a tangle. The research on that form scores it by crossings and wiggles, and ours had both everywhere.
  3. Frames side by side, not replay. In a tested comparison, people read change faster from static frames than from animation, and more accurately from small frames side by side. Animation was the weakest form for analysis; it suits presenting.
  4. Colour only for trouble. Kiali turns the failing box and line red, and Airflow's failed cells form a red block that you see before reading anything.
  5. Boxes linked to everything ruin a map. Process mining calls them "spider" activities and leaves them out or folds them in. In our harness, Work and the library are likely spiders.

1. Many things moving through one process, without drawing each one

Process mining map: thickness for frequency (Disco)

screens seen · help pages read

Good for us: the same data gives the unreadable "spaghetti" on the left, or on the right a map of about 10 boxes where thick dark arrows are the main flow and thin ones are rare. You see the main route and the loops without reading the numbers. The tool's own fix for spaghetti is two sliders: fewer boxes, and only the dominant paths.

Breaks with ours: 83 harness steps, with every move drawn, give the left picture. It needs the 19 pipelines or fewer, and only the main paths drawn.

fluxicon.com/book/read/simplification · fluxicon.com/book/read/mapview

"Spider" activities: one box linked to everything (Fluxicon)

screens seen · blog post read

Good for us: an activity that happens at any point (an email, a comment) sits in the middle with arrows from everywhere. Their post says these are "often among the least important" activities. Leaving them out turns the web on the left into the clear flow on the right.

Breaks with ours: every session goes back to Work between steps, and reads the library and notes at any time. Drawn as moves, those boxes become spiders. They have to be shown as a size, not as arrows.

fluxicon.com/blog/2015/03/managing-complexity-in-process-mining-part-iv-leaving-out-details

Flow map: width is the amount (Minard, 1869)

picture seen · its reputation from memory

Good for us: one band whose width is the size of the army along the route. You see where it shrank without reading a number. That is our "tokens went in, little landed" case: a wide band into Check and a thin one out to Land.

Breaks with ours: it tells one story along one route. Eight sessions at once are eight routes. It works only if they are added together into one flow.

upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/2/29/Minard.png

Traffic graph with moving dots (Netflix Vizceral)

screen seen · README read

Good for us: dots stream along the lines, and denser dots mean more traffic. It has three levels (global, region, service), and a click goes one level deeper.

Breaks with ours: the service level shown here (99 services) is a hairball. Only the global level, with a handful of boxes, reads. Netflix no longer uses it ("Not actively maintained").

github.com/Netflix/vizceral

2. Change over time on a diagram of boxes and lines

Storyline chart: a line per character over time (xkcd 657)

picture seen · Tanahashi & Ma 2012 abstract read · later work from search summaries

Good for us: where characters move in a few groups (Lord of the Rings, top), you can follow who was with whom.

Breaks with ours: the "Primer" panel (bottom right) is a knot, and that is round 5. The field scores these charts by line crossings and "wiggles" (jumps between rows). Eight sessions that change place every few minutes make both at every step. No layout fixes that.

xkcd.com/657 · ieeevis.org/year/2012/paper/infovis/design-considerations-optimizing-storyline-visualizations

Replay on the map (Disco animation, Kiali replay)

screens seen · help pages read

Good for us: cases move as dots along the map's arrows (Disco), and the dots are denser where traffic is higher (Kiali). Fluxicon says the animation is for showing results "to process owners or other people who are no process analysis experts". Kiali can replay a past stretch.

Breaks with ours: a replay shows one moment at a time, so you have to watch three hours to see where the work went. It does not work at a glance on a second screen.

fluxicon.com/book/read/mapview · kiali.io/docs/features/topology

Tests: frames side by side against animation

Robertson, Fisher, Lee, Stasko, Fernandez 2008 abstract read
"Animation is the least effective form for analysis; both static depictions of trends are significantly faster than animation, and the small multiples display is more accurate."

Archambault, Purchase, Pinaud 2011 search summary only: the open copy is behind a bot check
For graphs that change, small multiples (frames side by side) were faster than animation on every task they tested. Animation made fewer errors only on "what was added in this step". A 2016 follow-up found animation helps when the layout itself is unstable. Ours is fixed: it is his harness map.

For us: four frames of the same fixed map, one per stretch of time, beat a replay.

How big a node-and-line picture can be

Yoghourdjian et al. 2018, a survey of the tests on graph pictures paper's web version read:
"three quarters of studies use graphs with 100 nodes and 200 edges or less". The rest test showing only part of the graph at a time. In other words, nobody has shown that large node-and-line pictures read. Line crossings are a known cost to readability.

For us: the overview should stay well under that, at a dozen or so boxes, with the 83 steps only on zoom. That is what Disco, Kiali and Vizceral's global level do on screen.

3. What existing monitors of runs and pipelines look like

Service graph with health (Kiali)

screen seen · help page read

Good for us: about 15 boxes, and the failing box and its lines are red while the rest stay quiet. Moving dots show the load, with red diamonds for errors.

Breaks with ours: it shows one moment, with no history on the map.

kiali.io/docs/features/topology

Pipeline graph: stages as columns (GitLab)

screen seen · help page read

Good for us: stages left to right, jobs inside them, a tick or cross on each. One run reads at once.

Breaks with ours: it is one run. Eight sessions would be eight of these, which is round 2's "this session, this, that".

docs.gitlab.com/ci/pipelines

Grid of runs by step (Airflow)

screen seen · help page read

Good for us: one row per step and one column per run, with colour for state. The steps that keep failing form one red block that you see before reading.

Breaks with ours: it shows no flow from step to step. It is a table of cells, which you ruled out.

airflow.apache.org/docs/apache-airflow/stable/ui.html

Agent trace graph (Langfuse)

help page read · no picture found on the page

Good for us: in its default mode "repeated steps collapse into one node such as 'llm.chat (3/3)'". Many helpers doing the same thing become one box with a count.

Not looked at this round: metro maps, flight trackers, control-room display standards. Datadog and Temporal are known only from round 4's summaries.

langfuse.com/docs/observability/features/agent-graphs
