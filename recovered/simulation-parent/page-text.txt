Library pathways

A working model of the library design. A piece of work enters at the left and passes the design's steps. Each line is a way it can go. The thicker the line, the more work goes that way under the current settings.

There are no fixes on this page, and no judgment on whether the library reaches its goal. The page shows what the model gives under stated assumptions.

M
Measured on this PC, in the owner's repositories or session records.
S
Taken from an outside study or the vendor's price list.
G
A guess.

A number the model computes takes the weakest tag of its inputs. Every computed number here uses at least one guess, so it is tagged G. In a table or tree, the tag at the top applies to every number below it.

Words used on this page
Piece of work
One fix-sized task of about ten cheap calls, where a result is made and checked. The model files call it an episode.
Visible or latent
Visible: something signals a problem (an error, a failing check, a stated bug). Latent: nothing signals that a choice matters.
Entry
One stored conclusion in the library.
Look-alike
A stored entry that looks like a match but has a different cause.
Gate
Whatever decides a result is right: tests, and sometimes a review by Opus.
Ladder
What happens after a caught failure: one cheap retry, then Opus, then Fable.
Tier
A model level on the ladder: the cheap model (Sonnet), Opus, Fable.
Cost
Counted in cheap-call equivalents. One cheap-model call costs one. An Opus call costs the Opus price ratio.
Wrong rate
The share of pieces of work that do not end accepted and right: escaped (found later or never) or caught and never solved.
Stock
Something that piles up over time, drawn as a box. Here: good entries and bad entries.
Flow
What fills or drains a stock each period, drawn as a thick arrow with a valve.
Loop R or B
A chain of causes that returns to where it started. R (reinforcing) feeds itself. B (balancing) holds itself back.

SAME MISTAKE, NEW SESSION f01-C1a
Python is missing on this PC

A session found no usable python on this PC and wrote the fix down. Ten minutes after a compaction dropped the 'no Python' line, a top model typed a python heredoc again.

When: First fix 2026-10-06; repeat 2026-11-01. Source: Clinic session 1f10835f; session e24f17dc line 2928

The entry, as it would have been written
Python or python3 exits 49 (Microsoft Store stub). A real Python 3.12.14 sits in the Codex app folder. Call that full path, or use node or PowerShell for small JSON work. Check: the path with --version prints 3.12.14.
Step by step
STEP 1 · STRAINS
A task arrives: what is in the window. The window points toward Python, because CLAUDE.md names Python 3.12. A summary that said 'no Python' was replaced by one without it.
STEP 2 · NOT SPECIFIED
Trigger: does anything start a lookup, and when. The design names no trigger. A shell tool error is not a listed trigger. Only the exit-49 result could start a lookup, after the call.
STEP 3 · WORKS
Query: what is asked, in what words. The error text is word for word and easy to recognise.
STEP 4 · WORKS
Match: what comes back. One entry matches exactly.
STEP 5 · STRAINS
Delivery: which window receives it, and does it stay. This is the main session. The entry is held until the next compaction, and this session already lost the fact once at a compaction.
STEP 6 · WORKS
Use: the cheap model adapts and applies it. Swap python for the full path. A plain JSON script runs on 3.12.
STEP 7 · WORKS
Check: what decides the result equals the strong model's. The rerun's exit code decides, at no cost.
STEP 8 · NOT REACHED
Failure and the ladder: how failure is noticed, what goes up to Opus, then Fable. The failing model is already the top rung. A stronger model would make the same call.
STEP 9 · BREAKS
Capture: what is written, by whom, when, from what. Python was a side stumble. The design needs a fix commit and control pair, which a PC fact lacks. Memory got it 29 days later.
STEP 10 · BREAKS
Afterlife: what happens to the entry later. The interpreter belongs to another app, which rewrote its folder on 2026-10-21. Newer Pythons arrived later. No repository event marks any of this.

Limitations this case showed: LIM-27, LIM-41, LIM-51, LIM-58

R1
Bad entries bring more bad entries

Reinforcing: feeds itself.

More bad entries raise the share of bad entries a lookup returns. More wrong entries reach the cheap attempt and spoil work. More work climbs to Opus or Fable. More strong-tier solves and escapes get written, some of them bad.

R2
More entries, more look-alikes

Reinforcing: feeds itself.

Every entry, good or bad, raises the chance a misleading look-alike can be retrieved. Accepted look-alikes spoil work, more work climbs the ladder, and more entries get written.

B1
Good entries thin their own supply

Balancing: holds itself back.

More good entries fill the library. The cheap model is right more often with an entry in hand, so less work climbs to Opus or Fable, and fewer new entries are written.

B2
Good entries go stale

Balancing: holds itself back.

Each period a share of the good entries goes stale and moves to the bad stock. The more good entries there are, the more go stale.

What the map leaves out

The model's own limits, in plain words. They come from the model brief and from the independent check of the engine.

A model of the mechanism, not a forecast
Its numbers follow from its settings. 46M of the 55M unknowns that are drawn are guesses.
Chosen cases, not a sample of work
The 59M cases were chosen because something went wrong. They show which routes exist and where steps break. They never show how often.
Case statuses are judgments
Works, strains and breaks were set by the agents that walked the cases from the records. Another reader could set some differently.
One size of work
Every piece of work is treated as about ten cheap calls. Real work varies far more in size, and a large piece is not the same as many small ones.
Calls are the same size inside a tier
The cost unit is one cheap-model call. The price ratios are per token, so they equal the per-call ratio only if calls are the same size in every tier. On this PC's records an Opus call averaged about 222,000M cache-read tokens and a Sonnet call about 440,000M. On that data the per-call ratio would be near one, while the model fixes it at two.
Time and attention are not counted
Tests at the gate cost nothing. Waiting, wall-clock time and the owner's attention are left out. A lookup counts as calls; a mechanical check, carrying an entry and a handoff cost cheap-call equivalents but count as no calls.
Pieces of work are independent
A piece's kind, what the cheap model would do, what the library holds and whether a look-alike exists are combined by multiplying their chances. Real work clusters: a hard problem may also be the one the library lacks.
Averages, not one library
The two stocks change by their expected amounts each period. A real library has one history, with luck in it. Each period is worked out on the stocks at its start, and staleness acts on those starting stocks.
Entries are all-or-nothing
A good entry is good wherever it applies, and a bad one bad. Partly right shows up only as the composite 'some parts' result.
One pool of entries
Projects are not separated. Facts that differ between projects act only through the look-alike and wrong-entry chances.
A wrong entry acts once
A wrong accepted entry acts only at the cheap attempt. It does not follow the work up the ladder.
A fixed ladder and one gate
One cheap retry, then Opus, then Fable, in that order. Real sessions skip rungs, repeat them, or end with the owner. The chance the gate catches a wrong output does not depend on which model made it.
Bad entries stay bad
A bad entry turns good only when a found escape is repaired. Staleness moves good entries to bad, never the other way.
Two settings are fixed
The number of periods is a chosen horizon, never drawn. The Opus price ratio has a range of one value, so the runs never vary it.
Ranges are drawn evenly and on their own
If two unknowns move together in reality, the runs do not know it. The ranking measures steady rise or fall inside the ranges. It misses an effect that rises and then falls.
Some limitations act through nothing here
2M limitations name no unknown, so the model cannot show them: LIM-44 (An error line is not always a failure); LIM-68 (Private details travel with entries).
Some tags are generous
Several figures tagged M or S were measured on a different unit than the setting they stand for; each slider's source says how. One outside figure supports two settings in a row: the chance a wrong entry is accepted, and the chance it then spoils the work.
Two guards on impossible draws
If a draw makes 'all parts' plus 'some parts' exceed certainty, both are scaled down in proportion; the three ceilings on what the library can hold likewise. With the current ranges neither happens.
The step match is drawn by hand
The walkthroughs' ten steps and the model's stages are matched by a table, shown on the map. A limitation is placed on the map through that table and through its unknowns.
