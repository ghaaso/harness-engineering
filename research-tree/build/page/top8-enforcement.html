Method enforcement

Five rules that used to depend on a session remembering them are now checked by the machine and refused when broken. Each check was tried on real sessions or on past ones. A blind review found four faults that mattered, and all four are fixed.

Nothing is committed yet. It waits for your word.

What is enforced now

A session does not run past its hand-over point

Past its limit a session can still write its notes and hand over, and nothing else. This is the rule that top session 7 broke when it ran to 337.6 K.

Seen working live

No more helpers at once than the app allows

A start is refused when it would pass the app's limit of 20, or a width a plan declares with a number as its reason. The 20 limit itself was only replayed on the day it was broken; a width of zero was refused live.

Seen live for a declared width; the limit of 20 replayed

A helper's token limit rests on a measurement

A start must state its limit, the figure expected, and a measured basis, and all limits together must stay under the agreed cap. It also refuses a start unless the usage meter was read in an earlier message.

Seen working live

What reaches you is plain

Your closing box, a published page and the last message of a session are refused if they hold record ids, working names, file paths or money.

Box refusal seen live; the end-of-turn check not yet seen

Records go in through one tool

Every other way of writing to the journal is refused, shell commands included. A second check after each shell call reports any change made some other way, and the commit check refuses a line the tool did not write.

Refusal seen live; the after-the-call report is covered by tests only

What still cannot be enforced

Rules that need judgment: whether work is worth doing, whether a result is independent or general, whether a picture comes first or the words are plain.

Stays a written rule, checked by review

Why a call was made, and whether it was asked for or done on the session's own initiative. The two look alike in what a check can see.

Stays a written rule

Whether a record's status or claim is true. Only a reader can tell.
