Research run results: what to test first

Run 2026-11-harness-leads, judged 2026-11-23. Tokens are counted, not money. No figure of a lead was measured in the run. Test costs are computed or borrowed: A 3.2 M tokens a task-run, R 4.7 M a replay, P 384 M a paired pilot.

The answer

Test the cheap replays first. A replay over records already on disk costs about 4.7 to 9.4 M tokens and under 1 to 2 hours. None needs the graphics card, a download or an install. An agent pilot costs 192 to 389 M tokens, 40 to 80 times more, and no lead has evidence strong enough or close enough to our setting to justify a pilot before the replays size it.

The first wave is seven replays: about 37.6 M tokens expected, cap 87 M, each under 2 hours.

Quality (the tree)

  1. L11, accept "done" only against an executed check. Replay of helper DONE claims against gate and refuter verdicts. 4.7 M, under 1 h, card none. Source: GPT-5 submitted a patch on 100 % of runs and resolved 44 %; silent failures were 68 to 80 % of failing runs; two guard prompts left resolve unchanged (65.2 to 64.8 %). One author, preprint, 50 SWE-bench Verified tasks, 4 models, one scaffold. Acts on the 20-plus overstated reports and on hallucinations. Our own record agrees: model re-checks often failed in the testbed, executed checks did not.
  2. L8, type checker and lint gate. Replay the fix commits of S2 through tsc and mypy; first check whether Claude Code's diagnostics already run here. 4.7 M, under 1 h, card none. Source effect is small and old (15.0 to 18.0 % resolve, GPT-4 Turbo, one run per arm), but it acts on the largest counted quality sink (at least 5 P1 and 10 P2 defects made by fixes).
  3. L6, a reviewer that reads two versions. 9.4 M (40 reviewer calls), under 2 h, card none, on the 20-plus recorded overstatements. Source: loss with one faulty agent injected, hierarchical -5.51, flat -10.54, linear -23.72 points (GPT-3.5 and 4o, text tasks). That is not a detection rate. Generating the second version of each report costs a child run each.
  4. Later: D1 Phase A (L1), generated reproduction tests on the at least 15 fix-created defects, 70.5 M (15 R), card none. Phase B (256 M) only if Phase A catches a third or more.

Tokens: the router (the meter)

  1. L24 with L25, cache breaks and lapses. Scan per-call usage for cache writes that no first call, lapse or compaction explains, and for gaps over 300 s followed by writes. 4.7 M, under 1 h, card none. Cache writes are fresh tokens and fresh tokens drive the meter (a cached re-read weighs about 1 % of a fresh Opus token). Source: 108 of 120 prompt-caching defects in 10 agent projects raised no error (independent). Add to the same scan: first-call writes of helpers whose prefix a sibling wrote within five minutes.
  2. L2 with L27, the prefix per role and per helper start. 4.7 M for both, under 1 h, card none. The prefix is 27 % of context tokens (47 % in FixFlow), about 70,000 per fresh agent. In this run 301 helpers ran; the lightest took 35 to 52 K fresh tokens in all, so prefix writes were at most about 10 to 16 M of the run's 44.5 M fresh tokens (an upper reading). L2's pilot (384 M) only if the count shows a large fresh share.

Tokens: the graph

  1. L5, reads later falsified by an edit, and by whom. 4.7 M, under 1 h, card none, no model run. Source measured Claude Code with Opus 4.8 (48 SWE-EVO instances): 73.4 % of held tokens not needed by the next edit, 72.3 % of those stale; resolve up 2.7 to 4.9 points in all 18 cells (the maker's own study). Serves quality and tokens. Measure in the same scan what a drop costs: removing a held read mid-window rewrites the cache after it.
  2. D2 Phase A (L34, L35, L3), retriever accuracy of edited files from issue text on closed commits against BM25, grep and our RRF ranker. 4.7 M plus card hours, fits 12 GB. It needs the 0.55 GB or about 1.2 GB embedder weights and installs whose sources and sizes are not yet known (named to the owner first). Phase B (384 M) only past its gate. Expect little on our languages: the one Opus study's gain was mostly Go (47.1 against 29.9 %); Python 47.6 against 45.5 %; TypeScript untested.

Tokens: the library

  1. L38, how often recorded conclusions return, and which premises the traces miss. 4.7 M, under 1 h, card none. Our own replay found fact-level invalidation 0.00 % stale against 6.49 % at file level; no source estimates the return rate.
  2. L4's count goes inside L5's scan: in-window re-fetch here is estimated at 0.5 to 1.5 %, and in the source the reuse part alone raised input tokens on one model. The state view (current against stale) is the token lever.

Memory tier: nothing in the first wave uses the card; the 12 GB card enters at D2 Phase A; 24 GB only at D3. Four of the seven are among the run's ten replays (L11, L24-L25, L2-L27, L5); L8, L6 and L38 are outside them. The other six of the ten (L4 count, L7, L10, L16, L26, L41: 28.2 M) follow if the first wave leaves room. Local models (L23, L33, L49, L50, L55) fit 12 GB but act on cached re-reads that already cost about 1 % each on the meter. Time: L22 only. Memory: no lead reduces S9.
