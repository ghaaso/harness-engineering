What bigger cards can do for our models

25 November. A critical reading of every model in the round, from the code and the results so far. Nothing was run, downloaded or sent anywhere for it.

Most of our limits are not the card. Bigger models are worth buying in one place: getting the single right file to the very top of the list.

Finding files from a bug report

Near its ceiling in the top ten
  • Top ten: our learned ranker already has the right file there for about 92 % of reports, and the right file is among its candidates for nearly every report. There is little left to win.
  • Top two: only about half of reports. This is where the room is, and where a bigger model that re-orders the top results can help.
  • The clinic project: weakest of the three, because it has few past examples. That is a limit of the data. A bigger model does not add history.
  • What the 12 GB card forced: the two 7B models run squeezed to a quarter of their precision, bug reports are cut short before the re-ordering model reads them, and the search models train on short inputs in small batches.

Predicting files that change together

Limited by the signal, not by size
  • Size changes nothing: 22 graph-network settings of different sizes all scored within a hair of each other.
  • Text adds almost nothing: giving the model the files' text moved the score by a fifth of a point.
  • The time-aware models are below simple counting so far, as they are in the published record.
  • One cheap lever is left: the learned ranker was trained on only the latest tenth of the largest repository's history, for lack of time. That repository decides most of this score.

The five worth doing, best value first

1

A much larger re-ordering model (32B), at full precision

Aimed at the top two. Its authors report it clearly ahead of the 7B search model alone on their own benchmark. On our data it is untested.

Needs: a large download whose size and licence I check before asking you (its sibling models are for non-commercial use only), and credit.

3.6 GPU-hours
2

Our current 7B re-ordering model at full precision, reading whole bug reports

The control for number 1. It shows what the squeezing and the cutting cost us, so we know whether a gain comes from size or from precision.

Needs: its weights fetched on the rented machine (15 GB, non-commercial licence, the model we already use).

0.9 GPU-hours
3

The co-change ranker trained on all of the largest repository's history

All 1,044 blocks of history, where it used the latest 100. That repository decides most of the co-change score and gained least. Untested.

Needs: credit only.

1.0 GPU-hours, plus processor time
4

The 7B search model at full precision

The published figure for this model is at full precision. What the squeezing costs it on our data is unmeasured.

Needs: nothing new. The move already fetches this model.

1.0 GPU-hours
5

The search models retrained on longer inputs and bigger batches

They train on inputs half as long as the ones they are scored on, in batches of eight. Bigger batches are a known lever for this kind of training. Untested here.

Needs: credit. It has to wait for the current search-model training to finish.

4.0 GPU-hours

All five together: 10.5 GPU-hours on an RTX PRO 6000. Your balance covers the planned move with about 4 GPU-hours to spare, so numbers 1 and 2 fit; all five need a top-up of roughly two-fifths of your balance. Each one is added as a new entry with its own tuning and its own sealed-test files. Nothing already running is changed.

Not worth it here

  • A bigger graph network: size changes nothing, see above.
  • A huge text model feeding the co-change models: text adds a fifth of a point.
  • Bigger time-aware models: they are under simple counting, here and in the record.
  • Fine-tuning a 7B search model on our data: about thirteen times the cost of the current training, and the clinic project has under 200 examples.
  • Two other published methods that now fit in memory: one is weakest in exactly our case, the other has no measured figure in our records.

Free speed, same numbers

Each needs one exact-match check before it is used.

  • Several jobs per card: the biggest one, already in the plan.
  • Score the untrained baseline once, not again in every tuning stage.
  • Write saves less often.
  • Switch off a memory-saving mode one search model no longer needs on a big card.
