The two quick tests: what they found

25 November, on your PC, practice data only. How each would be judged was written down before it ran. The task: given one changed file, name the other files that change with it; the score is how many of the top ten picks are right.

The plateau came from what the models were given. New information moved the score; the graph network, in the form we built, added nothing to its own inputs.

How far each gets toward the best possible score

The full bar is the ceiling: the score of a perfect ranker (0.263).

  • Only about half the possible score is used. The problem is not at its limit.
  • One plain feature from new information, the words of the edit, is the first thing to clear our bar against counting (ahead by about 0.03; the bar is 0.02).
  • Files that never changed with this file before land in the top ten about twice as often (22 % against 11 %). Counting finds almost none of them.

  • Harder training examples hurt in this form.

What this does and does not settle

  • Settled: more size or a different training target for this one graph network is a dead end. New information is not.
  • Still open inside the history itself: a perfect ordering of the files that did change together before would score about 0.20; counting gets 0.124. The learned ranker picks up a little of that. Other graph forms were not tried here.
  • Where the learned ranker helps most: files with only a short history (one to five earlier changes). That is where a specialist would pay.
  • Limits: practice data, settings picked on that same data, two runs per setting at most. The gain from the edit's content comes mainly from the largest repository; the two small ones have too few cases to tell. The time-aware models were not part of these tests.

What the results point to building, in this order

1. Information first (design B)the edit's content in full and the code's structure: symbols, calls, routes
→
2. Then the judge (design A)a large model reads that evidence and must cite it; a checker removes anything made up
→
3. Then shrink ita small model learns from the judge

The judge needs evidence to read, and design B is what produces it, so B comes first. The router of design C goes inside B's ranker: the tests show where to split, by how much history a file has.
