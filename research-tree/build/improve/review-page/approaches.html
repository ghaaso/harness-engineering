Different approaches to the co-change problem

25 November. Argued from what we measured before, not from scratch. Nothing was run, downloaded or sent anywhere for it.

You were right. The plateau says our models all saw the same thin information, not that the problem is at its limit.

What the 22 settings really showed

  • Every model saw only what counting sees: which file was changed, the past co-changes, folders and imports, and weak text.
  • The graph network was handed the counts directly. Its 22 settings only varied the part that adds little, and their spread is within the noise of one run each. That test could not have shown an architecture effect.
  • Never tried: the content of the change itself, the code's structure (which function calls which, which symbol is defined where), harder training examples, or a different question.

What past results say

  • Five separate tests, one pattern: a model fed only the co-change history matched or fell below simple counting every time, whatever its kind or size.
  • Every gain we ever measured came from a new signal or from joining signals, never from a bigger model on the same input.
  • The earlier graph test: which candidates are reached mattered about fifteen times more than how they were ordered. Getting new information in beats re-ranking the old.
  • Counting already finds most of what history can find (roughly 77 to 92 % by repository). The room is in the files history cannot reach.

Design A: graph plus a large language model, with a checker

A pipeline of specialists. Aimed at fewer made-up answers: every pick has to cite real evidence.

Candidatescounting, search, code links
→
Learned rankerthe time-aware graph model is one of its inputs
→
Evidence cardsparsed facts about each candidate
→
Large judge model (32B)picks files and cites the evidence
→
Checker, no modeldrops any pick or cited link that does not exist
→
Then shrink ita small model (0.6B) learns from the judge

A made-up answer is counted before the checker: a picked file, a cited link or a cited change that did not exist at that time.
