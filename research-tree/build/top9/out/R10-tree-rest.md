## 1. What was built, run or measured, with its numbers

### 1.1 The protocol's design (P2): a design, not a run
- P2 status: "A draft; nothing starts before the owner's go" (P2 head, "Status") [design]. Its core is the owner's idea that the child who researched also reviews; the second expert and the 300,000 ceiling were the coordinator's additions, not his word (P2 head).
- Seven layers, each a borrowed solved design (P2 §1 table): execution (master-worker batch job, crash-only, admission reservation, watchdog), storage (append-only content-addressed store, views, truth maintenance), integrity (Clark-Wilson, Biba labels), evidence (rapid systematic review), assurance (seeded faults, acceptance sampling), shape and cost (B+ tree bulk load, square-root hand-over law, internal pilot), completeness (STPA, capture-recapture, model checking).
- Layout: run root outside every repository, own git repo, no CLAUDE.md (P2 §1 "Layout"); `tcb/` trusted scripts, `store/`, `views/`, `wt/<task>-e<e>/` attempt folders, `sealed/` seeds.
- Storage rules R13-R23 (P2 §3), the ones that serve quality and hallucination: R18 every sentence cites a pack and commit.mjs rejects uncited facts, cites outside the pack, and numbers equal to no cited value; R19 one label script (STALE > PENDING > JUDGMENT > IN), certificates void when the read-set hash changes; R22 read audit taints output whose transcript read outside declared inputs; R20 views compute the seven measures per task, field and arm.
- Integrity rules R24-R32 (P2 §4): only a script "witnesses" (quote and number found in a stored page); "confirmed" is a judgment whose detection rate is measured; checker reads only a queue batch and returns an enum; the ledger is hash-chained; fsck compares `tcb/` to the owner-named commit.
- Measured inputs on record, the only figures P2 §11 says exist: P = 79k per session, about 60k per agent; g = 2.8k per call (P2 §11 "Shape and completeness", pointing at the two files below).
  - Source of P and g, `docs/program/SPLIT-RULE-2026-11.md` "A floor under the line" (about lines 138-140) [measured]: a new session's first call reads a median 79k (75k to 88k by project group) over recorded FixFlow main sessions; an agent's first call 51k to 67k; the token report's figure is about 60k per agent, the register's TK-PREFIX about 70k. A window grows 2.8k a call (3.0k at the median window) (line 609, "A session window grows by 2.8k tokens per call") [measured].
  - `docs/program/TOKEN-BOTTLENECK-2026-11.md` line 44 [measured]: FixFlow had 1,118 agent windows of about 12 calls each; 56 % of every agent's tokens was its fixed prefix, about 60k, roughly 50k of it tool and connector schemas.
  - Caution: these are FixFlow transcripts (the product repository), not this repository's sessions.
- Split-rule replay (`SPLIT-RULE-2026-11.md` head, "The result in short") [measured exposure over transcripts, no model calls]: 72, 32 and 26 of 97 recorded session windows passed a line at 30, 40 and 50 % of a 1,000,000 window; the middle-case replay read 3.05B, 2.11B, 1.68B fewer context tokens than the 7.49B on record [estimate E], against 2.89B, 2.01B, 1.58B for an earlier compaction at the same line, i.e. 1.06, 1.05 and 1.06 times the cap; across nine stated cases the ratio ran 0.54 to 1.24, and "the record does not settle which is ahead on tokens". It also says Opus's 1,000,000 window is the weakest of the four assumptions (line ~125-130) and that a share of the window cannot be the whole rule because the floor (three times a new session's start, 237k to 264k) exceeds a 200,000 window.
