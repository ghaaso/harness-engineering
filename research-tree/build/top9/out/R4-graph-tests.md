## 1. What was built, run or measured

### 1a. Graph test (GT): which form, on which repository
- One repository only: the FixFlow product repo (June to August 2026 commits; GT "What was tested", "Not shown by this test": "Any other repository").
- Task: given one changed file of a real commit, which tool puts the other changed files and definitions in front of the reader within an 8,000-token answer. Recall = share of files/definitions the commit touched that the tool showed (a definition counts only with its own lines shown). GT "What was tested". [measured]
- Tools: typed store of code facts in three engines (SQLite relational, LadybugDB graph, DuckDB graph layer), a scripted text search (not an agent), a same-folder floor, a changed-together floor. Settings chosen on 65 tuning commits (June 2026), frozen; held-out run: 75 commits, 418 tasks, once. GT "What was tested".
- Held-out recall at 8,000 tokens (GT table "Held-out recall"): typed store 0.700 (all three engines identical), 4-hop variant 0.711, LadybugDB + community prior 0.716, scripted search 0.625 (best form 0.634), changed-together floor 0.638, same-folder floor 0.549. Tuning set: 0.785 typed, 0.759 search. [measured]
- Engines (GT item 1): identical answers, difference 0.000 [0.000, 0.000], identical on 770 of 770 tasks. [measured]
- Community prior (GT item 2): +0.015 [-0.010, +0.042]; no gain of 0.05 or more. [measured]
- Typed against scripted search (GT item 3): +0.076 [+0.036, +0.118], verdict "better". Mobile-app seeds +0.151 [+0.097, +0.207] (31 commits); outside the mobile app +0.024 [-0.023, +0.075] (48 commits). Mobile seeds were 17 % of tuning tasks and 36 % of held-out tasks. Against changed-together floor +0.063 [+0.019, +0.110] (outside mobile +0.001 [-0.051, +0.056]); against same-folder floor +0.151 [+0.092, +0.207]. 48 % of the search arm's credited recall comes from its changed-together padding tail. [measured]
- What moved the score (GT item 4, tuning, "optimistic"): hop depth 0.346 (0.438 at 1 hop to 0.784 unbounded); direction 0.215; ranking modes at most 0.023. [measured]
- Code-reading questions (GT item 5, 30 held-out): F1 typed minus search +0.100 [-0.060, +0.257], inconclusive. Final answers median 31 vs 29.5 tokens; scripted search read median 694.5 tokens; typed side's own costs (schema, query programs) not counted. [measured]
- Engine speed (GT item 6, tuning inputs only): warm median per ranking SQLite 14.5 ms / 32.7 ms (tuning / frozen graph), LadybugDB 41.5 / 71.2, DuckDB 70.3 / 98.7. A model step takes about 6.6 s, so engine speed does not enter the token bill. [measured]

## 2. Results that decide a choice, and what must not be ruled out

### 2a. Typed store vs search (GT) [measured]
- The typed store beats scripted search on recall at equal answer size on this repository, but the lead lives in mobile-app seeds (+0.151) and not outside them (+0.024, interval crosses zero) (GT item 3). It is a result for one form (a scripted search, not an agent; ranked file lists, not an agent's loop) on one repository.
- It is a recall-at-8,000-tokens result, not "an agent makes fewer mistakes". Tokens in sessions, quality and hallucination effect of any of these tools: untested (GT item 7).
- Engine choice is null for answers (identical on 770 of 770); the engine only matters for speed and size, which "do not enter the token bill" (GT item 6). GV section G: graph databases, reachability indexes, vertex reordering pay at about 10^5 to 10^7 nodes [hypothesis; the design's own thresholds, not measured here].
- Community prior: null for this form (GT item 2); GV section G lists Leiden communities and GraphRAG community summaries as low priority.
- Graphify code layer: typed beat it by -0.137 here; graphify's own gain claim (71.5x, GT section K) is self-reported, weak; its document layer is untested (GT section M "Not shown").

### 2b. Embeddings (EC): exactly what is and is not ruled out
Reading the report gives: "No arm adds to the simple ranker on this repository" and "the trained embeddings mostly recover the co-change facts they were trained on; below those they order candidates about as well as rooted PageRank... fused with the simple ranker they add nothing this test's bar could register (at most about +0.006 in pool P@10, where the protocol asked for +0.02)" (EC section 4 end, section 1). The reasons (EC section 4):
- The models learn (mean reciprocal rank 0.68 to 0.75 vs 0.28 for PPR on held-out co-change pairs), but PPR's graph has no co-change edges, so this shows learning, not beating a ranker that has them (point 1).
- Alone they separate known co-change partners from other candidates almost perfectly (AUC 0.996 to 1.000); inside that block they do not follow CO's order (rank correlation 0.07 to 0.29); below it they are as accurate as PPR (P@10 0.196 to 0.213 vs 0.208) (point 2).
- More weight does not help: weights 2, 4, 8 outside the grid, no gain above +0.006 in the pool (point 4).
- The pool "matches" is a weak comparison: the fixed fusion (k = 60) keeps co-change candidates above others in 224 to 226 of 226 testable tasks, the fused top ten shares 9.1 to 9.3 of 10 files with the simple ranker, and the placebo moves the list as much as real models. A perfect score through the same selection would gain +0.060, so "matches" rules out "a third to a half of the gain that could be reached, not all of it" (point 5). The arms alone, unrestricted lists and higher weights carry the conclusion.
- E1 gain not excluded: its interval nearly excludes zero (lower end -0.0002); on the 46 shared commits it lies wholly above zero (+0.0031 [+0.0008, +0.0057]); gain at most about +0.006 pool, about +0.007 unrestricted (EC section 1, section 3 "Three things").

What the record says must NOT be ruled out (EC sections 6 and 7; GV section G):
1. Temporal or inductive models, text or code embeddings, rule learners, larger dimensions, longer history: "Not tested" (EC section 7).
2. The routed-specialist idea: the test used folders as areas, RotatE specialists and the protocol's merge. Most of E5's shortfall comes from the merge as written (about 60 % of its gap to E1 on two-area tasks); on 121 single-area tasks E5 equals E1 (0.2226 vs 0.2219). The choice of second area (the documentation-heavy "other" area on 153 of 313 tasks) and the quality of specialists were not separated; the test "says nothing about routing on a graph too large for one model" (EC section 4 point 6, section 7).
3. E2 ComplEx-N3's regulariser was divided by three (paper's formula) vs the authors' code (no factor); the chosen setting 0.033 is below both published values (0.1 for WN18RR, 0.05 for FB15k-237); nothing stronger was tried; "Whether a stronger N3 would change E2's 'matches' cannot be told" (EC section 6 item 9).
4. Other training departures with no ruling: initial values the library's; negatives not filtered against known facts (the authors' code filters them, per the auditor's recall, not checked); selection always took the smallest model because the standard error (about 0.022) exceeded every difference between settings (at most 0.015) (EC section 6 items 10, 11). JH section 4 lists "the best training regime of KG embeddings (whether it changes the earlier embeddings null, x09-0306)" as a key the research run did not cover.
5. Scale: the verdict is for about 1,600 to 2,450 nodes, 6 facts per node; GV section G says KG embeddings "pay at about 300 usable commits and 10x edges, tuned and multi-seed only" [hypothesis; the design's own threshold, not measured]. The repository has about 233 commits at head (EC section 6 item 8).
6. File-level truth: "what a commit edited, not what it needed to read" (EC section 7; GT "Not shown": context that a change needed but did not edit).
7. Text embeddings as a retriever (not KG embeddings) were never run: GT "Possible next tests" names "An embedding-search arm (needs a model download)"; JH L34, L35 are the leads for it (section 5 below). One null is not a verdict: this is one form (KG embedding models fused into the rooted-PageRank plus co-change ranking) on one repository.
