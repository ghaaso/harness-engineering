**Required cases:**
- **Balance sequence:** a 1000 invoice less a 400 payment leaves 600 due, a return of 100 without credit still leaves 600, and a recognized credit of 100 leaves 500.
- **Allocation:** a payment across two invoices with an advance reconciles exactly.
- **Stock:** a credit, refund or replacement never duplicates stock.
- **Fault injection:** a fault at any step of any of the ten S06 commands rolls the whole command back.
- **Closed period:** each of those commands is refused on a closed posting date.
- **Access:** every S06 URL refuses anonymous and unauthorized callers.
- **Control accounts:** the supplier totals tie to them.

**Races and lock removal:** four two-connection races each name the row lock that decides them and show the second call waiting on it: the invoice row, the credit's row, the supplier row, and the credit's document. I removed each lock in turn and re-ran its race, then restored the files exactly:
- Without the invoice or credit row lock, the database's deferred check still refuses the over-allocation, but as a raw database error instead of the proper message.
- Without the supplier-row lock, both links commit and the return is settled twice.
- Without the document lock, a link commits on a credit that was just reversed.

**Opus review:** it found 13 issues and no way to over-pay or over-allocate. I fixed 11. The serious ones:
- a link could commit on a credit being reversed at the same moment;
- a return could be "replaced" by the delivery it came from;
- a 1-unit credit could settle every returned unit;
- some reference and variance checks existed only in the service, not the database;
- the cost estimate went stale after an invoice was reversed.

Two are recorded rather than changed: price differences are not treated as variances, and each document type has a single reversal permission.
